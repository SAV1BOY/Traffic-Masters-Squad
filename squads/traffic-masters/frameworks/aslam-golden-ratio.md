# Golden Ratio Content Framework
> **Author**: Kasim Aslam / Content Strategy
> **Domain**: Content Marketing, Ad Creative Mix, Competitive Markets
> **Used by agents**: content-strategist-agent, creative-strategist-agent, media-buyer-agent

## Overview
A content distribution formula for competitive markets: 60% educational content,
30% social proof, 10% direct offer. This ratio builds trust and authority before
asking for the sale. In crowded markets where every competitor is running direct
response ads, the Golden Ratio differentiates through value-first positioning.

## When to Use
- Entering competitive markets where prospects are ad-fatigued
- Building brand authority alongside direct response campaigns
- When direct-response-only approach is producing diminishing returns
- Content planning for social media, email, and ad creative mixes

## The Framework
### 60% Educational Content
- Teach something genuinely useful without asking for anything
- Types: How-to videos, industry insights, tips, frameworks, tutorials
- Goal: Position the brand as the expert and trusted resource
- Distribution: Blog posts, social media, YouTube, email newsletters
- Key: Must be high quality — mediocre education erodes trust

### 30% Social Proof Content
- Show results, testimonials, case studies, and transformations
- Types: Customer stories, before/after, review compilations, data
- Goal: Build credibility through evidence, not claims
- Distribution: Ads, social media, landing pages, email
- Key: Specificity wins — "grew revenue 43%" > "got great results"

### 10% Direct Offer Content
- Make the ask: buy, book a call, sign up, enroll
- Types: Promotional ads, sales pages, limited offers, launch content
- Goal: Convert the audience you have already educated and proven to
- Distribution: Retargeting ads, email sequences, sales pages
- Key: The offer should feel like a natural next step, not a hard pivot

## Key Concepts
- The ratio creates a trust flywheel: educate → prove → convert → repeat
- 60% education means your audience grows even without buying
- 30% social proof pre-sells before the offer is ever made
- 10% direct offer converts at higher rates because trust is established
- The ratio applies to content volume, not necessarily ad spend
- In less competitive markets, the ratio can shift toward more direct offer

## Decision Rules
- IF market is highly competitive → strict 60/30/10 ratio
- IF brand is already well-known → can shift to 50/30/20
- IF launching a new product → temporarily shift to 40/30/30
- IF educational content is not driving engagement → improve quality first
- IF social proof is weak → invest in gathering testimonials and case studies
- IF direct offer CVR is high → the education and proof are working

## Common Mistakes
- Producing low-quality educational content that damages credibility
- Using social proof that is vague or unverifiable
- Making every piece of content a direct offer (the 0/0/100 mistake)
- Not tracking which educational content drives eventual purchases
- Applying the ratio rigidly without adjusting for business stage
