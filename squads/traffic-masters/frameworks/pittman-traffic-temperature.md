# Traffic Temperature - Cold/Warm/Hot Audience Segmentation

> **Author**: Molly Pittman
> **Domain**: Audience Strategy & Funnel Mapping
> **Used by agents**: traffic-strategist, media-buyer, funnel-architect
> **Checklists**: audience-segmentation-checklist, temperature-mapping-worksheet

## Overview

Traffic Temperature is Molly Pittman's foundational audience segmentation model. Every person who could see your ad exists at one of three temperatures: Cold, Warm, or Hot. Each temperature requires fundamentally different messaging, offers, creative, landing pages, and budget allocation. Treating all traffic the same is the single most common and costly mistake in paid acquisition.

## When to Use

- Planning any new campaign (temperature mapping is always step 2 of the Traffic Engine)
- Diagnosing why a campaign is underperforming (often a temperature mismatch)
- Building audience segments in any ad platform
- Deciding what offer to show which audience
- Allocating budget across funnel stages

## The Framework

### Cold Traffic

**Definition**: People who have never heard of you. They do not know your brand, your product, or why they should care. They are strangers.

**Mindset**: "Who are you and why should I pay attention?"

**Objective**: Awareness and value delivery. Earn the right to continue the conversation.

**Messaging approach**:
- Lead with the problem or desire, not your product
- Educate and provide value before asking for anything
- Use social proof from recognizable sources
- Avoid jargon or insider language

**Offer types**: Lead magnets, free content, quizzes, blog posts, ungated video, free tools

**Creative approach**: Emotion and Average Day hooks perform best. Video tends to outperform static for cold. UGC builds trust faster than polished brand creative.

**Landing pages**: Long-form with education, proof stacking, and low-friction CTAs (email opt-in, not purchase)

**Budget allocation**: 60-70% of total ad spend typically goes to cold traffic to fill the top of funnel

**Benchmarks**: Higher CPC, lower CTR, lower conversion rate -- this is expected and acceptable

### Warm Traffic

**Definition**: People who know you but have not bought. They have consumed your content, visited your site, engaged with your social, downloaded a lead magnet, or opened your emails. They are acquaintances.

**Mindset**: "I know you, but I'm not sure you can help me specifically."

**Objective**: Nurture the relationship and build trust. Move them closer to a buying decision.

**Messaging approach**:
- Reference their previous interaction ("You downloaded our guide...")
- Provide deeper proof (case studies, testimonials, demos)
- Address specific objections
- Show the transformation your product delivers

**Offer types**: Webinars, tripwire offers, free trials, product demos, case study content

**Creative approach**: Average Day and Logic hooks work well. Retargeting creative should feel personal, not generic. Carousel formats showing results or process steps perform well.

**Landing pages**: Medium-length with proof stacking, testimonials, and clear value proposition. Can include moderate-friction CTAs (registration, small purchase).

**Budget allocation**: 20-30% of total ad spend

**Benchmarks**: Moderate CPC, higher CTR than cold, higher conversion rate

### Hot Traffic

**Definition**: People who are ready to buy or have bought before. They have added to cart, visited pricing pages, started checkout, or are past customers. They are friends or existing customers.

**Mindset**: "I want this. Give me a reason to act now."

**Objective**: Close the sale or generate repeat purchases. Remove final friction.

**Messaging approach**:
- Be direct about the offer
- Lead with urgency and scarcity (genuine, not manufactured)
- Remind them of what they are missing
- For past customers, introduce complementary offers

**Offer types**: Core offers with bonuses, limited-time pricing, upsells, cross-sells, loyalty offers

**Creative approach**: Logic hooks dominate. Direct response copy. Clear pricing and terms. Testimonials from similar buyers. Dynamic product ads for e-commerce.

**Landing pages**: Short, focused sales pages or direct-to-checkout. Minimal friction. Trust badges, guarantees, and FAQ to handle last objections.

**Budget allocation**: 10-20% of total ad spend

**Benchmarks**: Lowest CPC, highest CTR, highest conversion rate, lowest CPA

## Key Concepts

- **Temperature is relative to your brand**: Someone cold to you may be warm to your competitor
- **Temperature changes over time**: Cold becomes warm through engagement, warm becomes hot through nurture
- **Never skip temperatures**: Sending cold traffic to a sales page is like proposing on a first date
- **Retargeting is temperature management**: Pixel-based audiences let you serve the right message at the right temperature
- **Budget follows the funnel shape**: Wide at top (cold), narrow at bottom (hot)

## Decision Rules

- IF conversion rate is low on a cold campaign THEN check if you are asking too much too soon (wrong offer for temperature)
- IF warm retargeting CPA is higher than cold THEN your nurture content is not building enough trust
- IF hot traffic is not converting THEN the offer or landing page has friction issues -- not a traffic problem
- IF you are only running hot traffic campaigns THEN your funnel will dry up as you exhaust the audience
- IF budget is limited THEN prioritize warm retargeting first (highest ROI), then hot, then cold

## Common Mistakes

- Running the same ad to all temperatures -- one-size-fits-all messaging fails
- Spending 90% of budget on cold traffic with no warm/hot follow-up -- leads go cold and waste
- Ignoring past customers (hot) -- the cheapest sale is to someone who already trusts you
- Defining warm too broadly -- a 180-day website visitor is not truly warm anymore
- Not refreshing retargeting creative -- warm audiences see the same ad 20 times and tune out
