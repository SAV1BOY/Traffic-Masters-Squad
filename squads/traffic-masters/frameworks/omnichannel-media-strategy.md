# Omnichannel Media Strategy Framework
> **Author**: Universal / Media Planning
> **Domain**: Multi-Channel Strategy, Media Mix, Channel Selection
> **Used by agents**: strategist-agent, media-buyer-agent, campaign-planner-agent

## Overview
A framework for how Meta, Google, YouTube, TikTok, and LinkedIn work together in a
cohesive media strategy. Defines channel selection criteria, the role each channel
plays in the funnel, and the recommended expansion sequence. No channel operates in
isolation — the strategy is the orchestration.

## When to Use
- Planning a multi-channel media strategy from scratch
- Deciding when and how to expand from one channel to multiple
- Allocating budget across channels based on strategic role
- When single-channel performance is declining and diversification is needed

## The Framework
### Channel Roles and Strengths
#### Meta (Facebook/Instagram)
- Role: Primary demand generation and retargeting
- Strengths: Audience building, broad reach, visual creative, lookalikes
- Funnel fit: All stages (TOFU through BOFU)
- Best for: B2C, e-commerce, lead gen, courses, apps

#### Google Search
- Role: Demand capture — intercept active intent
- Strengths: High-intent queries, measurable, keyword targeting
- Funnel fit: MOFU and BOFU (search intent = consideration/purchase)
- Best for: Services, SaaS, local, high-ticket, e-commerce (Shopping)

#### YouTube
- Role: Storytelling, education, awareness at scale
- Strengths: Long-form video, intent targeting, Google ecosystem
- Funnel fit: TOFU and MOFU (awareness and consideration)
- Best for: Courses, SaaS, complex products, brand building

#### TikTok
- Role: Discovery and viral awareness
- Strengths: Organic-feeling creative, younger demos, creative testing
- Funnel fit: TOFU primarily, some MOFU
- Best for: B2C, e-commerce, trend-driven products, mobile-first

#### LinkedIn
- Role: B2B targeting and professional audiences
- Strengths: Job title, company, industry targeting precision
- Funnel fit: TOFU and MOFU for B2B
- Best for: B2B, SaaS, professional services, recruiting

### Channel Selection Criteria
1. Where does the target audience spend time?
2. Does the channel match the offer type and price point?
3. Does the team have creative capabilities for the channel format?
4. Is the minimum viable budget available for this channel?
5. Does this channel fill a gap in the current strategy?

### Expansion Sequence (Recommended)
1. Start with ONE channel — typically Meta or Google based on business type
2. Master that channel: achieve profitable, consistent results
3. Add channel #2: typically the complement (Meta + Google or vice versa)
4. Add YouTube for storytelling/education or TikTok for discovery
5. Add LinkedIn if B2B or niche professional targeting is needed
6. Never add a channel until the previous one is stable and profitable

## Key Concepts
- Channels are not competitors — they serve different roles in the customer journey
- Meta generates demand; Google captures demand; YouTube educates; TikTok discovers
- The sum is greater than the parts: multi-channel creates reinforcement effects
- Expand only when current channels are profitable and stable
- Cross-channel attribution is imperfect — use MER for strategic decisions
- Each channel requires channel-specific creative — do not repurpose blindly

## Decision Rules
- IF B2C product → start with Meta, add Google Shopping
- IF B2B/SaaS → start with Google Search, add LinkedIn
- IF video content is a strength → prioritize YouTube early
- IF audience is Gen Z → prioritize TikTok
- IF budget < $5k/month → one channel only until profitable
- IF adding a channel → allocate minimum 10% of budget for 90-day test

## Common Mistakes
- Spreading budget across too many channels too early
- Repurposing Meta creative for YouTube without adaptation
- Adding channels before the primary channel is profitable
- Not accounting for cross-channel attribution when evaluating performance
- Treating each channel team as independent instead of coordinated
