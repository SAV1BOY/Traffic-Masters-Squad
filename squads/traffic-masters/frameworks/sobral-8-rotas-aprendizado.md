# 8 Rotas de Aprendizado Framework
> **Author**: Pedro Sobral / Traffic Specialization Paths
> **Domain**: Career Development, Specialization, Traffic Management
> **Used by agents**: training-agent, strategist-agent, career-development-agent

## Overview
Eight specialization routes for traffic managers after mastering fundamentals. Each
route represents a distinct market with its own dynamics, metrics, creative needs, and
client expectations. Traffic managers should choose a primary route to specialize in
rather than being generalists. Depth in one route beats shallow knowledge of all eight.

## When to Use
- Guiding new traffic managers toward a specialization
- Assessing which route fits a traffic manager's skills and interests
- Structuring a team to cover multiple routes with specialists
- When a generalist approach is producing mediocre results across the board

## The Framework
### Route 1: Trafego Local (Local Business)
- Clients: Restaurants, clinics, gyms, local services
- Platforms: Google Ads (local), Meta (geo-targeted), Google Business Profile
- Key skills: Local targeting, call tracking, foot traffic measurement
- Metrics: Cost per call, cost per visit, local impression share
- Challenge: Small budgets, high client expectations, many clients needed

### Route 2: E-commerce
- Clients: Online stores, D2C brands, retail
- Platforms: Meta, Google Shopping, PMax, TikTok
- Key skills: Product feeds, ROAS optimization, catalog ads, remarketing
- Metrics: ROAS, AOV, cost per purchase, cart abandonment rate
- Challenge: Thin margins, seasonal variation, feed management

### Route 3: Infoprodutos (Digital Products)
- Clients: Course creators, coaches, digital educators
- Platforms: Meta (primary), Google (secondary), YouTube
- Key skills: Evergreen funnels, VSL traffic, webinar registration
- Metrics: Cost per lead, cost per sale, funnel conversion rates
- Challenge: Market saturation, creative fatigue, compliance

### Route 4: Lancamentos (Launches)
- Clients: Product launchers, seasonal campaigns
- Platforms: Meta (primary), Google, YouTube
- Key skills: Phase-based budgeting, urgency campaigns, list building
- Metrics: Cost per registration, show-up rate, launch ROAS
- Challenge: Boom-bust cycles, high pressure, compressed timelines

### Route 5: Apps
- Clients: App developers, mobile-first businesses
- Platforms: Google App campaigns, Meta, Apple Search Ads
- Key skills: App install tracking, in-app events, retention metrics
- Metrics: Cost per install, cost per action, LTV:CAC, retention rates
- Challenge: Attribution complexity, app store optimization integration

### Route 6: B2B
- Clients: SaaS companies, professional services, enterprise
- Platforms: LinkedIn, Google Ads, Meta (for remarketing)
- Key skills: Lead scoring integration, ABM, long sales cycles
- Metrics: Cost per MQL, SQL, pipeline value, deal velocity
- Challenge: Small audiences, high CPCs, long conversion windows

### Route 7: Google/YouTube Specialist
- Clients: Any vertical, but through Google/YouTube exclusively
- Platforms: Google Search, Shopping, Display, YouTube
- Key skills: Keyword strategy, video ads, bid management, feed optimization
- Metrics: Quality score, impression share, view rate, tCPA/tROAS
- Challenge: Platform complexity, constant updates, competitive auctions

### Route 8: International
- Clients: Global brands, multi-market companies
- Platforms: All major platforms across regions
- Key skills: Multi-language campaigns, cultural adaptation, currency management
- Metrics: Performance by market, localized CPA targets, global ROAS
- Challenge: Cultural nuance, time zones, platform availability by region

## Key Concepts
- Specialize in one route before expanding to a second
- Each route has unique rhythms, metrics, and client relationships
- The best route depends on market opportunity, personal interest, and local demand
- Routes 1-3 are the most common starting points for new traffic managers
- Routes 6-8 typically require more experience and command higher fees
- Skills from one route transfer partially to others

## Decision Rules
- IF new traffic manager → start with Route 1 (Local) or Route 3 (Infoprodutos)
- IF strong analytical skills → consider Route 2 (E-commerce) or Route 7 (Google)
- IF creative skills → consider Route 3 (Infoprodutos) or Route 4 (Lancamentos)
- IF B2B experience → Route 6 is the natural fit
- IF multilingual → Route 8 (International) is a competitive advantage

## Common Mistakes
- Trying to serve all routes simultaneously without depth in any
- Choosing a route based on perceived revenue instead of genuine interest
- Not studying the specific dynamics of the chosen route
- Applying strategies from one route to another without adaptation
- Switching routes too frequently without developing expertise
