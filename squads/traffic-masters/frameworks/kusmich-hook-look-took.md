# Hook-Look-Took

> **Author**: Nicholas Kusmich
> **Domain**: Ad Creative Structure
> **Used by agents**: copywriter, creative-strategist, media-buyer
> **Checklists**: ad-creation-checklist, creative-review-checklist

## Overview

Hook-Look-Took is Nicholas Kusmich's three-part ad creation framework. Every effective ad accomplishes three things in sequence: Hook (stop the scroll), Look (keep them consuming), and Took (drive them to act). Each part must be strong on its own AND flow seamlessly into the next. A great Hook with a weak Look loses attention. A great Look with no Took wastes the engagement earned.

## When to Use

- Writing any ad creative — copy, video scripts, image-based ads
- Reviewing underperforming ads to identify which part is failing
- Training creative teams on ad structure fundamentals
- Creating ad templates and frameworks for repeatable production
- Adapting one concept across multiple formats (text, image, video, carousel)

## The Framework

### Part 1: Hook (What Stops the Scroll)

**Visual Hook (the image or first frame)**
1. Pattern interrupt: Break the visual monotony of the feed
2. High contrast, bold elements, or unexpected imagery
3. Faces perform well — especially with direct eye contact or strong emotion
4. Text overlay should be minimal but impactful (max 5-7 words)
5. Must be immediately legible on mobile at small size

**Text Hook (the first line of copy)**
1. Must create an instant reaction: curiosity, recognition, or surprise
2. Formulas that work:
   - Question: "Are you still [doing painful thing]?"
   - Bold claim: "[Number]% of [audience] get this wrong"
   - Story opening: "Two years ago, I was [relatable situation]..."
   - Direct address: "If you're a [specific person] who wants [outcome]..."
   - Contrarian: "Stop [common advice]. Here's what actually works."
3. Length: 1-2 sentences maximum. Front-load the impact.
4. The hook must pass the "so what?" test — would someone care enough to keep reading?

**Video Hook (first 3 seconds)**
1. Start with motion, emotion, or a provocative statement
2. Never start with a logo, intro sequence, or "Hey guys..."
3. Show the most interesting moment first — then back up and explain
4. Text on screen in the first 2 seconds for sound-off viewers
5. The first 3 seconds determine whether 80%+ of viewers stay or leave

### Part 2: Look (What Makes Them Read/Watch)

**Building curiosity and sustaining attention**
1. Open a loop: Introduce a question, tension, or story that needs resolution
2. Deliver value: Teach something useful — a tip, insight, or reframe
3. Create emotional engagement: Use stories, examples, or relatable scenarios
4. Build credibility: Weave in proof points, data, or expertise naturally
5. Maintain momentum: Each sentence or scene should pull them to the next

**Structure options for Look**
- **Story arc**: Setup > tension > turning point > resolution lead-in (CTA)
- **Listicle**: 3-5 specific tips or insights, each building on the last
- **Problem-solution**: Articulate the pain (empathy) > reveal the solution (hope)
- **Case study**: Real example with specific details and measurable outcomes
- **Myth-busting**: Common belief > why it is wrong > what to do instead

**Length guidelines**
- Facebook feed: 3-7 sentences for primary text (longer can work if the Look is strong)
- Instagram: Shorter copy, visual storytelling carries the Look
- Video ads: 30-90 seconds for most offers (longer for complex/high-ticket)
- The Look should be as long as it needs to be and no longer

### Part 3: Took (What Action Do They Take)

**CTA design**
1. Be specific about the action: "Click the link below to download your free guide"
2. Reinforce the value: "Get your free [magnet] and start [desirable outcome] today"
3. Reduce risk: "No credit card required" / "Free instant access" / "100% guarantee"
4. Create urgency only if genuine: "Limited spots" / "Offer ends Friday"
5. Match CTA to audience temperature:
   - Cold: "Learn more" / "See how" / "Discover"
   - Warm: "Get your free guide" / "Watch the training" / "Join the challenge"
   - Hot: "Buy now" / "Start today" / "Claim your spot"

**Took amplifiers**
- Social proof near the CTA: "Join 12,000+ [audience] who already..."
- Future pacing: "Imagine [desirable outcome] just [timeframe] from now"
- Contrast: "You can keep [painful current state] or [take this simple action]"
- Simplicity: "All it takes is one click"

## Key Concepts

- **Sequential dependency**: Hook earns the Look. Look earns the Took. Skipping stages fails.
- **Hook is the most testable element**: Same Look and Took with different Hooks = easy testing
- **Look is where trust is built**: This is the substance. Without it, Hooks feel like clickbait.
- **Took must feel earned**: If the Hook and Look did their job, the Took feels natural
- **Mobile-first design**: 80%+ of views are mobile — design every element accordingly

## Decision Rules

- IF impressions are high but CTR is low THEN the Hook is failing — test new visual or text hooks
- IF CTR is high but time on page is low THEN the Look did not deliver on the Hook's promise
- IF engagement is high but click-through to CTA is low THEN the Took is weak or missing
- IF video view rate drops at 3 seconds THEN the video Hook needs to be stronger
- IF video view rate drops at 25% THEN the Look is losing attention — tighten the middle
- IF ad comments are positive but conversions are low THEN the Took is not clear enough

## Common Mistakes

- Spending all effort on the Hook and neglecting the Look (clickbait without substance)
- Burying the Hook beneath preamble or context (get to the point immediately)
- Making the Took an afterthought — one weak sentence at the end
- Mismatching Hook tone with Look tone (bait-and-switch within the ad)
- Using generic CTAs ("Click here") instead of value-specific CTAs
- Not testing Hook variations — the Hook has the highest impact per change
