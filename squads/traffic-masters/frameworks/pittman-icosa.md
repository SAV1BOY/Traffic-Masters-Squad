# ICOSA - Social Media Content Strategy

> **Author**: Molly Pittman
> **Domain**: Social Media Strategy & Content-to-Ad Pipeline
> **Used by agents**: content-strategist, social-media-manager, creative-strategist
> **Checklists**: icosa-content-calendar, content-to-ad-pipeline-checklist

## Overview

ICOSA is Molly Pittman's 5-stage framework for social media content strategy: Ideation, Creation, Optimization, Scheduling, and Amplification. While it applies to organic social media, its real power lies in connecting organic content performance to paid advertising strategy. Organic content is the testing ground; paid amplification scales what works. ICOSA creates a systematic pipeline from content idea to high-performing ad.

## When to Use

- Building or rebuilding a social media content strategy
- Creating a content-to-ad pipeline to fuel paid campaigns
- Identifying which organic posts should become paid ads
- Planning a content calendar that serves both organic and paid goals
- Diagnosing why social content is not generating business results

## The Framework

### Stage 1: Ideation

Generate content ideas systematically, not randomly.

**Sources for ideation**:
- Customer questions (support tickets, DMs, comments, sales calls)
- Competitor content (what is working for others in your space)
- Industry trends and news (timely content outperforms evergreen in social)
- Customer avatar pain points and desires (from avatar research)
- Product use cases and transformations (real results, real stories)

**Process**: Maintain a running idea bank. Score ideas on three criteria:
1. Relevance to avatar (1-5)
2. Potential for engagement (1-5)
3. Connection to a paid offer (1-5)

Prioritize ideas scoring 12+ across all three criteria.

### Stage 2: Creation

Produce content in batches for efficiency.

**Content types that feed paid strategy**:
- Educational posts (demonstrate expertise, build trust)
- Story-based posts (customer transformations, behind-the-scenes)
- Data-driven posts (statistics, results, benchmarks)
- Opinion/contrarian posts (stand for something, create conversation)
- UGC and testimonial posts (social proof at scale)

**Production rules**:
- Batch create 1-2 weeks of content at once
- Create multiple formats per idea (text post, image, video, carousel)
- Front-load the hook in every piece (first line of text, first 3 seconds of video)
- Include a clear next step even in organic content

### Stage 3: Optimization

Refine content before and after publishing.

**Pre-publish optimization**:
- Platform-specific formatting (aspect ratios, character limits, hashtag strategy)
- Hook testing: write 3 versions of the opening line, pick the strongest
- Visual hierarchy: ensure the key message is visible without clicking "see more"

**Post-publish optimization**:
- Monitor engagement in first 60 minutes (early engagement signals algorithmic boost)
- Respond to every comment in the first hour
- Track which content types, topics, and hooks generate the most engagement

### Stage 4: Scheduling

Publish at the right time, in the right sequence.

**Scheduling principles**:
- Use platform analytics to identify peak engagement windows
- Alternate content types (do not post three educational posts in a row)
- Maintain consistent frequency (3-5 posts per week minimum per platform)
- Schedule around key business events (launches, promotions, seasonal moments)
- Leave room for reactive/timely content (do not schedule 100% in advance)

### Stage 5: Amplification

This is where organic meets paid. The amplification stage turns top-performing organic content into paid ads.

**The content-to-ad pipeline**:
1. Identify organic posts with above-average engagement (2x your average)
2. Analyze why they worked (hook, topic, format, timing)
3. Repurpose as paid ads with minimal modification
4. Use the Ad Grid (pittman-ad-grid-7-steps.md) to create variations
5. Test amplified content against purpose-built ads
6. Feed learnings back into Stage 1 ideation

**Amplification budget rule**: Start with 10-20% of ad budget on amplified organic content. Scale based on performance.

## Key Concepts

- **Organic is the testing ground**: Let organic engagement validate ideas before spending ad dollars
- **Content-to-ad pipeline**: The systematic process of turning organic winners into paid winners
- **Hook-first creation**: Every piece of content, organic or paid, lives or dies by its hook
- **Batch production**: Creating content one piece at a time is inefficient; batch by content type or topic
- **Engagement velocity**: Early engagement signals platform algorithms to distribute content wider

## Decision Rules

- IF an organic post gets 2x average engagement THEN consider it for paid amplification
- IF a content type consistently underperforms THEN reduce its share of the calendar
- IF engagement is declining THEN audit hooks and topics, not just posting frequency
- IF amplified organic outperforms purpose-built ads THEN shift more budget to amplification
- IF a topic generates high engagement but no business results THEN it is attracting the wrong audience

## Common Mistakes

- Treating organic and paid as completely separate strategies with no data sharing
- Creating content without considering whether it could become an ad
- Posting without hooks -- burying the lead in social content is invisible content
- Optimizing for vanity metrics (likes) instead of business metrics (clicks, leads, sales)
- Inconsistent posting frequency -- algorithms reward consistency
- Never amplifying organic winners -- leaving proven content performance on the table
