# 4Ms - Campaign Foundation

> **Author**: Nicholas Kusmich
> **Domain**: Campaign Planning and Strategy
> **Used by agents**: strategy-orchestrator, media-buyer, copywriter
> **Checklists**: campaign-planning-checklist, pre-launch-checklist

## Overview

The 4Ms framework is Nicholas Kusmich's campaign foundation model. Before launching any campaign, four elements must be defined and aligned: Message (what are you saying), Market (who are you saying it to), Magnet (what irresistible offer draws them in), and Mechanism (how does it all work together). Misalignment in any single M leads to campaign failure regardless of how well the other three are executed.

## When to Use

- Planning any new campaign from scratch
- Diagnosing why an existing campaign is underperforming
- Client onboarding to establish campaign foundations
- Before writing ad copy or building funnels
- Quarterly campaign reviews to check alignment
- When scaling campaigns that were built without clear foundations

## The Framework

### M1: Message (What Are You Saying?)
1. **Clarity**: Can the prospect understand the message in 3 seconds?
2. **Specificity**: Does the message address a specific problem for a specific person?
3. **Value proposition**: What unique benefit does this message promise?
4. **Differentiation**: How does this message stand apart from competitors?
5. **Emotional resonance**: Does the message connect to a feeling, not just a feature?
6. Message hierarchy:
   - Primary message: The one thing you want them to remember
   - Supporting messages: Evidence and details that reinforce the primary
   - Proof messages: Social proof, data, testimonials that validate

### M2: Market (Who Are You Saying It To?)
1. **Primary market**: The core audience most likely to buy
2. **Secondary market**: Adjacent audiences worth testing
3. **Targeting precision**: Specific interests, behaviors, demographics, custom audiences
4. **Audience temperature**: Cold, warm, or hot — determines message approach
5. **Market awareness level**: Unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware, product-aware, most-aware
6. Market validation questions:
   - Is this market large enough to sustain the campaign?
   - Can you reach this market on the chosen platform?
   - Does this market have purchasing power for this offer?
   - Is this market actively seeking solutions?

### M3: Magnet (What Is the Irresistible Offer/Lead Magnet?)
1. **Value delivery**: Does the magnet solve a real, immediate problem?
2. **Specificity**: Is it targeted to a specific audience with a specific outcome?
3. **Quick win**: Does it deliver a result fast (ideally within minutes)?
4. **Consumption**: Is it easy to consume (short, clear, actionable)?
5. **Bridge**: Does it naturally lead to the paid offer?
6. Magnet types by effectiveness:
   - Checklists, cheat sheets, templates (high consumption, high conversion)
   - Video training, webinars (medium consumption, high conversion)
   - Ebooks, guides (low consumption, medium conversion)
   - Free trials, demos (high intent, high conversion)
7. The $20 test: Would someone pay $20 for this? If not, improve it.

### M4: Mechanism (How Does It Work?)
1. **The funnel**: What is the step-by-step path from ad to customer?
2. **Traffic flow**: Ad > Landing page > Thank you page > Email sequence > Offer
3. **Technology**: What platforms and tools power each step?
4. **Follow-up**: What happens after they opt in? (Email, retargeting, SMS)
5. **Conversion points**: Where in the mechanism does money change hands?
6. **Friction audit**: Where might people drop off? How can you reduce friction?
7. Mechanism validation:
   - Does every step have a clear purpose and clear next action?
   - Is the mechanism mobile-optimized?
   - Has each page been load-speed tested?
   - Are tracking and attribution properly configured?

### The Alignment Check
1. Does the Message match what the Market cares about?
2. Does the Magnet deliver on the promise of the Message?
3. Does the Mechanism smoothly deliver the Magnet and transition to the offer?
4. Would the Market find the entire experience coherent and trustworthy?
5. If any alignment is broken, fix it before spending on ads.

## Key Concepts

- **Alignment over excellence**: A perfectly crafted Message to the wrong Market will fail
- **The Magnet is the bridge**: It converts advertising interest into a relationship
- **Mechanism is invisible**: The best funnels feel effortless to the prospect
- **Message-Market fit first**: Get this right before investing in Magnet and Mechanism
- **Sequential dependency**: Define M1 and M2 first; M3 and M4 follow from them

## Decision Rules

- IF CTR is low THEN Message-Market alignment is off — either the message or the targeting is wrong
- IF opt-in rate is low THEN the Magnet is not compelling enough for this Market
- IF opt-in rate is high but sales are low THEN the Mechanism (funnel/nurture) is broken
- IF all metrics are decent but scale is limited THEN the Market is too narrow
- IF a campaign worked then stopped THEN the Market may have shifted — re-evaluate M2
- IF competitors are winning with similar offers THEN your Message lacks differentiation

## Common Mistakes

- Starting with the Mechanism (building funnels) before defining Message and Market
- Creating a Magnet that appeals to freebie-seekers rather than buyers
- Targeting too broadly (Market) because the Message is not specific enough
- Assuming the Mechanism works without testing each step independently
- Changing multiple Ms simultaneously, making it impossible to diagnose issues
- Skipping the alignment check and launching with mismatched elements
