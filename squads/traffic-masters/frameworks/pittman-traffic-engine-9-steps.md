# The Traffic Engine - 9-Step System

> **Author**: Molly Pittman

> **Used by agents**: traffic-strategist, media-buyer, campaign-planner

## Overview

The Traffic Engine is Molly Pittman's foundational 9-step system for building a repeatable, scalable traffic machine. Most advertisers treat traffic as a random activity -- running ads, hoping something sticks, and reacting to results. The Traffic Engine replaces randomness with a systematic process where each step feeds the next. When all 9 steps are in place, you have a machine that reliably converts ad spend into customers.

## When to Use

- Starting a new paid traffic initiative from scratch
- Auditing an existing campaign that is underperforming
- Onboarding a new client and need a structured plan
- Training a team on the fundamentals of paid acquisition
- Rebuilding a campaign after a major pivot or offer change

## The Framework

### Step 1: Determine Your Customer Avatar

Define who you are trying to reach with specificity. Go beyond demographics. Identify their goals, values, challenges, pain points, and objections. A campaign without a clear avatar is a campaign without direction.

Example: "Sarah, 35, marketing manager at a SaaS company, struggling to prove ROI on ad spend, reads Marketing Brew, follows Gary Vee, fears wasting budget."

### Step 2: Identify Your Traffic Temperature

Classify your audience into Cold, Warm, or Hot (see pittman-traffic-temperature.md). This determines everything downstream -- your messaging, offer, creative, and landing page. Never serve the same message to all three temperatures.

### Step 3: Choose Your Traffic Source

Select the platform(s) that match your avatar and temperature. Options include Meta (Facebook/Instagram), Google Ads, YouTube, TikTok, LinkedIn, Pinterest, native ads, and more. Start with one platform and master it before expanding.

### Step 4: Create Your Lead Magnet

Build a value-first asset that solves a specific problem for your avatar. The lead magnet is the bridge between cold traffic and your funnel. It must be immediately useful, easy to consume, and directly connected to your paid offer.

### Step 5: Build Your Landing Page

Design a dedicated page optimized for a single conversion action. No navigation bars, no distractions. The page must match the ad that sent the visitor there (congruence). Headline, sub-headline, bullet points, social proof, CTA.

### Step 6: Design Your Ad

Create ads using the Ad Grid (see pittman-ad-grid-7-steps.md) and Hook Framework (see pittman-hook-framework.md). The ad must stop the scroll, communicate relevance to the avatar, and compel the click. Match ad messaging to traffic temperature.

### Step 7: Set Up Your Tracking

Install pixels, configure conversion events, set up UTM parameters, and verify that data flows correctly before spending a dollar. Without tracking, optimization is impossible.

### Step 8: Optimize Your Campaign

Monitor performance daily. Adjust bids, audiences, placements, and creative based on data. Kill underperformers quickly. Double down on what works. Use MPI indicators (see burns-mpi.md) to guide decisions.

### Step 9: Scale What Works

Once you have a winning combination (avatar + offer + creative + landing page), scale it. Increase budget gradually (20-30% every 3-5 days). Expand to new audiences and platforms. Replicate the system for new offers.

## Key Concepts

- **Systematic over random**: Every step has a purpose and feeds the next step
- **Temperature-aware**: The same traffic source can serve cold, warm, and hot -- but never with the same message
- **Tracking as foundation**: Steps 7-9 are impossible without proper tracking infrastructure
- **Lead magnet as bridge**: Cold traffic rarely buys directly; the lead magnet creates the relationship

## Decision Rules

- IF you cannot clearly define your avatar THEN stop and do customer research before proceeding
- IF your lead magnet conversion rate is below 20% THEN fix the lead magnet before scaling traffic
- IF your ad CTR is below platform benchmarks THEN revisit Step 6 and test new hooks
- IF CPA is rising after scaling THEN slow the budget increase and check for audience saturation
- IF tracking shows data gaps THEN pause spend and fix tracking before continuing

## Common Mistakes

- Skipping Step 1 and targeting "everyone" -- broad targeting without avatar clarity wastes budget
- Using the same ad for cold and hot traffic -- temperature mismatch kills conversion rates
- Launching without proper tracking -- you cannot optimize what you cannot measure
- Scaling too fast -- jumping budget 3x overnight causes algorithm instability
- Ignoring the lead magnet -- sending cold traffic directly to a sales page rarely works
- Optimizing ads before fixing the landing page -- the click is only half the equation
