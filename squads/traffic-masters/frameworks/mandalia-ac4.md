# AC-4 Formula

> **Author**: Depesh Mandalia
> **Domain**: Campaign Diagnostics and Optimization
> **Used by agents**: media-buyer, performance-analyst, strategy-orchestrator
> **Checklists**: performance-diagnostic-checklist, optimization-checklist

## Overview

The AC-4 Formula is Depesh Mandalia's diagnostic and optimization model expressed as R = EM x AC4-squared. It provides a systematic way to identify what is working and what is broken in any campaign. The formula captures the reality that small improvements across multiple levers compound into significant results, while the Emotional Multiplier (Brand layer) amplifies everything.

## When to Use

- Campaign results are declining and you need to diagnose the root cause
- Planning a new campaign and want to ensure all levers are addressed
- Performance review meetings to structure the analysis
- When one metric is off but you are unsure which upstream variable is responsible
- Prioritizing optimization efforts across multiple campaigns

## The Framework

### The Formula: R = EM x AC4-squared

### EM — Emotional Multiplier (The Brand/Emotional Layer)
1. How strong is the emotional connection with the audience?
2. Does the brand have recognition, trust, and positive sentiment?
3. Is there a story or mission that makes people care beyond the product?
4. EM acts as a multiplier — a strong EM amplifies all AC4 variables
5. A weak EM (approaching 0) makes even great AC4 produce poor results

### AC4 — The Four Levers

**Lever 1: Product**
- Is the product genuinely good? Does it solve a real problem?
- Product-market fit: does the market actually want this?
- Unique mechanism: what makes this product different?
- Social proof: reviews, testimonials, case studies

**Lever 2: Audience**
- Are you reaching the right people?
- Targeting precision: interests, behaviors, lookalikes, custom audiences
- Audience temperature: cold, warm, hot — matched to the right message
- Audience saturation: have you exhausted this pool?

**Lever 3: Offer**
- Is the offer compelling enough to drive action?
- Value stack: what does the customer get vs. what do they pay?
- Risk reversal: guarantees, free trials, money-back promises
- Urgency and scarcity: legitimate reasons to act now

**Lever 4: Funnel**
- Does the funnel convert traffic into customers efficiently?
- Landing page: does it match the ad promise?
- Load speed, mobile optimization, UX clarity
- Follow-up sequence: email, retargeting, SMS

### The Squared Effect
- Small improvements across all 4 levers compound exponentially
- A 10% improvement in each lever does not yield 40% — it yields much more
- This is why broad optimization beats single-variable fixation

## Key Concepts

- **Diagnostic power**: When results drop, systematically check each AC4 variable before panicking
- **Compounding**: The squared relationship means marginal gains across all levers beat large gains in one
- **EM as multiplier**: Without emotional connection, even perfect AC4 underperforms
- **Isolation testing**: Change one variable at a time to identify which lever moved the needle
- **Hierarchy**: Fix Product first, then Audience, then Offer, then Funnel — in that order

## Decision Rules

- IF ROAS is dropping across all campaigns THEN check Product (market shift) or EM (brand perception)
- IF CTR is low THEN the Audience or creative (Offer presentation) is misaligned
- IF CTR is high but conversion rate is low THEN the Funnel or Offer is broken
- IF CPA is good but volume is low THEN Audience is too narrow — expand the pool
- IF a winning campaign suddenly dies THEN check Audience saturation first, then creative fatigue
- IF everything looks right but results are mediocre THEN EM is likely weak — invest in brand

## Common Mistakes

- Blaming the platform algorithm when the issue is one of the AC4 levers
- Optimizing Funnel when the real problem is Product-market fit
- Ignoring the Emotional Multiplier because it is harder to measure
- Changing multiple levers simultaneously, making it impossible to attribute improvement
- Over-optimizing one lever while neglecting the others (diminishing returns)
- Not establishing baseline metrics before making changes
