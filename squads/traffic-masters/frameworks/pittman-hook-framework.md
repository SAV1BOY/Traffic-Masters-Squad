# Hook Framework - 3 Hook Types for Ad Creation

> **Author**: Molly Pittman
> **Domain**: Ad Copywriting & Creative Strategy
> **Used by agents**: ad-copywriter, creative-strategist, media-buyer
> **Checklists**: hook-writing-checklist, ad-copy-review-checklist

## Overview

The Hook Framework is Molly Pittman's system for creating the most critical element of any ad: the hook. The hook is the first line of ad copy, the first 3 seconds of video, or the headline on a static image. It determines whether someone stops scrolling or keeps moving. There are three hook types -- Average Day, Emotion, and Logic -- and each maps to specific traffic temperatures and audience states. This framework provides templates and decision rules for selecting and writing hooks.

## When to Use

- Writing ad copy for any platform (Meta, Google, YouTube, TikTok, LinkedIn)
- Creating video scripts (the hook is the opening)
- Designing static ad images (the hook is the headline text)
- Populating the Ad Grid with hook variations per segment
- Diagnosing low CTR or poor engagement on existing ads

## The Framework

### Hook Type 1: Average Day

Paint a vivid picture of the audience's daily experience. This hook works by creating instant recognition -- the reader thinks "that's me" and stops scrolling.

**Structure**: Describe a specific moment in their day that connects to the problem you solve.

**Template variations**:

1. "Picture this... you wake up and [specific morning scenario related to the problem]"
2. "It's 3pm on a Tuesday and you're [specific frustrating activity]..."
3. "You just finished [routine task] and you realize [painful truth]..."
4. "Every morning you open your laptop and the first thing you see is [frustrating metric/situation]..."
5. "Remember the last time you [common experience]? What if that never happened again?"
6. "You're scrolling through [platform] and you see [competitor/peer] doing [thing they want to do]..."
7. "It's Sunday night and you're already dreading [Monday scenario]..."

**Best for**: Warm traffic. These hooks work when the audience already knows the problem exists but needs to feel seen and understood before engaging with a solution.

### Hook Type 2: Emotion

Lead with a feeling. These hooks tap into the audience's emotional state -- fear, desire, frustration, hope, pride, or shame. They bypass rational thinking and create immediate resonance.

**Structure**: Name or evoke the emotion directly, then connect it to the problem or desire.

**Template variations**:

1. "Tired of [frustrating situation that won't change]?"
2. "What if you never had to worry about [fear] again?"
3. "Stop wasting [time/money/energy] on [thing that doesn't work]..."
4. "You deserve better than [current painful situation]..."
5. "Imagine the freedom of [desired outcome] without [current sacrifice]..."
6. "The embarrassment of [specific painful moment] ends today."
7. "Finally -- [desired outcome] without [common objection/barrier]."
8. "Don't let [fear] stop you from [aspiration] one more day."
9. "What would change if you could [achieve specific result] in [timeframe]?"
10. "The secret [successful group] won't tell you about [desired outcome]..."

**Best for**: Cold traffic. Emotion hooks are the most effective for reaching people who do not know you yet. The emotional resonance earns the right to continue the conversation.

### Hook Type 3: Logic

Lead with data, statistics, rational arguments, or proven results. These hooks appeal to the analytical mind and work when the audience already trusts you enough to evaluate evidence.

**Structure**: Present a compelling fact, statistic, or logical argument that demands attention.

**Template variations**:

1. "[Specific number]% of [group] are making this mistake with [topic]..."
2. "We spent $[amount] testing [thing] -- here's what actually works."
3. "3 reasons why [common approach] is costing you [specific loss]..."
4. "The data is clear: [specific finding with numbers]."
5. "[Number] out of [number] [professionals] agree that [contrarian point]..."
6. "We analyzed [large number] of [data points] and found [surprising insight]..."
7. "Here's the math: [simple calculation showing ROI or loss]."
8. "In [timeframe], our clients saw [specific measurable result]. Here's how."
9. "Study: [credible source] found that [relevant statistic about the problem]."
10. "The #1 reason [desired outcome] fails? [Data-backed answer]."

**Best for**: Hot traffic. Logic hooks work when the audience already knows and trusts you. They are evaluating whether your specific solution is the right choice. Data and proof close the deal.

## Key Concepts

- **The hook is the gatekeeper**: No matter how good your offer or landing page, if the hook fails, nobody sees the rest
- **3 seconds or less**: On social platforms, you have 3 seconds (video) or one glance (image/text) to earn attention
- **One hook per ad**: Do not try to combine all three types. Each ad leads with one hook type
- **Hook-body-CTA structure**: The hook stops the scroll, the body builds the case, the CTA directs the action
- **Test hooks, not just ads**: The fastest way to improve ad performance is to test new hooks while keeping the body and CTA constant

## Decision Rules

- IF targeting cold traffic THEN use Emotion hooks (70% of cold creative should be emotion-led)
- IF targeting warm traffic THEN use Average Day hooks (they feel personal and build connection)
- IF targeting hot traffic THEN use Logic hooks (proof and data close the decision)
- IF CTR is below 1% on Meta THEN the hook is failing -- test 3 new hooks before changing anything else
- IF a hook type wins consistently THEN create 5+ variations of that type (not 5 variations of the losing types)
- IF video view-through rate drops at 3 seconds THEN the hook is not strong enough
- IF an ad has high CTR but low conversion THEN the hook is making a promise the landing page does not deliver

## Common Mistakes

- Writing hooks that are clever but unclear -- clarity beats creativity every time
- Using the same hook type for all temperatures -- mismatched hooks underperform
- Burying the hook after a preamble -- the hook IS the first line, no warm-up
- Writing hooks about yourself ("We are excited to announce...") instead of about the audience
- Using hooks that attract curiosity-seekers instead of buyers -- clickbait damages conversion
- Not testing enough hooks -- most advertisers test 2-3 when they should test 10-15
