# Creative Lab - Deep Research Process for Ad Creation

> **Author**: Ralph Burns
> **Domain**: Creative Research & Brief Development
> **Used by agents**: creative-strategist, ad-copywriter, research-analyst
> **Checklists**: creative-lab-research-checklist, creative-brief-template

## Overview

The Creative Lab is Ralph Burns' 7-14 day deep research process that happens before a single ad is created. Most advertisers skip research and jump straight to writing copy or shooting video. The Creative Lab ensures that every ad concept is built on real customer language, proven angles, and genuine market insight. The output is a creative brief loaded with ammunition -- not assumptions.

## When to Use

- Starting a new client engagement (always begin with Creative Lab)
- Launching a new offer or product line
- Current creative has plateaued and needs fresh angles
- Entering a new market or targeting a new avatar segment
- Every 30-60 days as part of the Kaizen Kreative cycle (Phase 1)

## The Framework

### Research Stream 1: Customer Research (Days 1-5)

Go where customers talk about their problems in their own words.

**Sources**:
- **Customer interviews**: Talk to 5-10 recent buyers. Ask: "What was happening in your life when you decided to buy? What almost stopped you? What result did you get?"
- **Reviews**: Read 50+ reviews of your product and competitors. Mine the 3-star reviews -- they contain the most balanced, specific feedback.
- **Forums and communities**: Reddit, Facebook groups, Quora, niche forums. Search for threads about the problem you solve.
- **Support tickets**: Read the last 100 support tickets. What questions keep coming up? What frustrations exist?
- **Sales call recordings**: Listen to 10+ calls. What objections arise? What language do prospects use?
- **Social media comments**: Read comments on your posts and competitors' posts. What resonates?

**What to capture**:
- Exact phrases and words customers use (verbatim quotes)
- Specific pain points (not "they want more money" but "they check their bank account every morning with dread")
- Desired outcomes in their language
- Objections and fears about buying
- Triggers that made them act

### Research Stream 2: Competitor Analysis (Days 3-7)

Study what competitors are doing in paid media, and identify gaps.

**Sources**:
- **Meta Ad Library**: Search competitors and analyze their active ads. Note: what hooks are they using? What formats? What offers? How long have specific ads been running (longevity = performance)?
- **Google Ads Transparency Center**: View competitor search and display ads
- **TikTok Creative Center**: Analyze trending ads and competitor activity
- **Spy tools**: Tools like AdBeat, SpyFu, or SimilarWeb for deeper competitive intelligence
- **Landing page analysis**: Click through competitor ads and document their full funnel

**What to capture**:
- Hooks that competitors are using repeatedly (repetition signals performance)
- Angles they are NOT using (gaps = opportunities)
- Offers and pricing structures
- Creative formats and production styles
- Landing page approaches and conversion mechanisms

### Research Stream 3: Market Narrative (Days 5-10)

Understand the conversation happening in the market right now.

**Questions to answer**:
- What is the dominant narrative in this market? (e.g., "AI will replace marketers")
- What are people afraid of right now?
- What recent events, trends, or news items affect your audience?
- What beliefs does your audience hold that you can align with or challenge?
- What is the contrarian take that would stop the scroll?

**Sources**: Industry publications, social media trends, podcast conversations, Google Trends, news aggregators.

### Research Stream 4: Angle Mining (Days 7-12)

Synthesize research into advertising angles.

**The four angle categories**:

1. **Pain angles**: Lead with the problem. "Still struggling with [specific pain]?"
2. **Desire angles**: Lead with the outcome. "Imagine [specific desired result]..."
3. **Objection angles**: Address the reason they have not bought. "Think [common objection]? Here's why that's wrong."
4. **Proof angles**: Lead with evidence. "[Specific result] achieved by [specific person] in [specific timeframe]."

For each category, generate 5-10 specific angles drawn directly from research. Every angle must trace back to a real customer quote, review, or data point.

### Research Stream 5: Hook Ideation (Days 10-14)

Transform angles into specific hooks ready for ad creation.

**Process**:
1. Take each angle and write 3 hooks (one per hook type: Average Day, Emotion, Logic)
2. Score each hook on: specificity (1-5), emotional resonance (1-5), uniqueness (1-5)
3. Prioritize top-scoring hooks for production
4. Map hooks to traffic temperatures

**Output format**: Each hook should include the verbatim customer language that inspired it, making it easy to verify the hook is grounded in reality.

## Key Concepts

- **Research before creation**: The quality of the ad is determined before a word of copy is written
- **Customer language is the creative**: The best ad copy is often a direct quote from a customer
- **Verbatim capture**: Paraphrase loses power. Capture exact words, phrases, and emotional language.
- **Angles from evidence**: Every angle must trace back to a research data point, not a marketer's assumption
- **Gaps are opportunities**: What competitors are NOT saying is often more valuable than what they are saying

## Decision Rules

- IF you cannot find 50+ customer reviews or comments THEN extend research to adjacent competitors
- IF customer language contradicts your assumptions THEN trust the customer language
- IF a competitor has run the same ad for 90+ days THEN that angle is validated -- study it, then differentiate
- IF an angle appears across multiple research streams THEN it is high priority
- IF research reveals a pain point nobody is addressing THEN this is your highest-value creative angle

## Common Mistakes

- Skipping research and writing ads from the marketer's perspective instead of the customer's
- Reading reviews without capturing verbatim quotes -- you lose the specific language that converts
- Only researching your own customers -- competitor reviews and forums reveal blind spots
- Treating research as a one-time event instead of a recurring phase every 30-60 days
- Mining angles without mapping them to specific hooks and traffic temperatures
- Ignoring the market narrative -- timely ads outperform generic ads
