# Contextual Congruence

> **Author**: Nicholas Kusmich
> **Domain**: Ad Creative and Platform Strategy
> **Used by agents**: copywriter, creative-strategist, media-buyer
> **Checklists**: creative-review-checklist, platform-adaptation-checklist

## Overview

Contextual Congruence is Nicholas Kusmich's framework for making ads feel native to the platform where they appear. Ads that look and feel like interruptions get ignored or resented. Ads that match the social conversation happening on the platform get consumed naturally. The core test: "Would a friend share this?" If the answer is no, the ad lacks congruence.

## When to Use

- Creating new ad creative for any platform
- Adapting existing creative for a new platform
- When CTR or engagement rates are below platform benchmarks
- Reviewing creative for "ad blindness" — ads that look too much like ads
- Training creative teams on platform-native content creation
- When frequency is acceptable but engagement is dropping (content fatigue, not audience fatigue)

## The Framework

### The Congruence Principle
1. Every platform has an implicit social contract — a type of content users expect
2. Ads that honor this contract feel native; ads that break it feel intrusive
3. The goal is not deception — it is respect for the user's context
4. Congruent ads earn attention; incongruent ads demand it (and fail)

### Platform-Specific Congruence

**Facebook Feed**
- Context: Personal updates, life events, opinions, shared articles
- Congruent creative: Personal stories, conversational copy, "I just discovered..." framing
- Format: Text-heavy posts with authentic images, native video (not polished commercials)
- Tone: Like a friend sharing something helpful or interesting
- Avoid: Stock photos, corporate language, "limited time offer" banners

**Instagram Feed**
- Context: Visual inspiration, aesthetics, lifestyle, curated moments
- Congruent creative: Beautiful imagery, aspirational visuals, clean design
- Format: High-quality photos, carousels with visual storytelling, short Reels
- Tone: Aspirational but approachable, visually led with minimal text overlay
- Avoid: Text-heavy images, low-quality visuals, overly promotional captions

**Instagram Stories/Reels**
- Context: Raw, authentic, behind-the-scenes, quick entertainment
- Congruent creative: Phone-shot video, face-to-camera, unpolished and real
- Format: Vertical video, quick cuts, captions for sound-off viewing
- Tone: Casual, immediate, personal — like a friend's story
- Avoid: Horizontal video, overly produced content, long intros

**LinkedIn**
- Context: Professional development, industry insights, career growth
- Congruent creative: Thought leadership, data-driven insights, professional advice
- Format: Long-form text posts, document carousels, professional video
- Tone: Authoritative but conversational, insight-led
- Avoid: Hard selling, overly casual language, irrelevant personal stories

**YouTube**
- Context: Learning, entertainment, deep-dive content
- Congruent creative: Valuable tutorials, reviews, demonstrations, storytelling
- Format: Horizontal video, clear audio, structured content with hook
- Tone: Expert teacher, entertainer, or storyteller
- Avoid: Short clips that feel out of place, ads that interrupt without adding value

### The Friend Test
1. Look at the ad in context — on the platform, in the feed, among other content
2. Ask: "Would a friend share this with me?"
3. Ask: "Does this feel like content I would stop to consume naturally?"
4. Ask: "Would I be embarrassed if my name was attached to this ad?"
5. If any answer is no, the ad lacks congruence — revise it

### Congruence Spectrum
- **Full congruence**: Looks and feels like organic content (highest engagement)
- **Partial congruence**: Feels like content but has obvious commercial intent (acceptable)
- **Low congruence**: Clearly an ad but not obnoxious (functional but inefficient)
- **No congruence**: Screams "advertisement" and interrupts (wastes budget)

## Key Concepts

- **Attention is earned, not taken**: Congruent ads earn the scroll-stop; intrusive ads get scrolled past
- **Platform respect**: Each platform has a culture — understand it before advertising on it
- **Authenticity over production value**: A genuine phone video often outperforms a polished commercial
- **Context changes everything**: The same message needs different creative treatment per platform
- **Congruence is not deception**: You are not hiding that it is an ad — you are making it worth consuming

## Decision Rules

- IF CTR is below platform average THEN check congruence first — the ad likely looks like an ad
- IF engagement (likes, comments, shares) is low THEN the content does not fit the platform conversation
- IF the same creative works on Facebook but fails on Instagram THEN it lacks visual congruence for IG
- IF video completion rate is low THEN the video style does not match platform viewing behavior
- IF ad comments are negative ("this is an ad" / "stop selling") THEN congruence is severely lacking
- IF organic content outperforms ads significantly THEN study the organic style and adapt

## Common Mistakes

- Using the same creative across all platforms without adaptation
- Over-producing ads to the point they feel like TV commercials on social feeds
- Using stock photography on platforms that value authenticity
- Writing ad copy in "marketing speak" instead of conversational language
- Ignoring platform-specific format requirements (aspect ratios, length limits)
- Prioritizing brand guidelines over platform congruence (your brand guide was not written for social feeds)
