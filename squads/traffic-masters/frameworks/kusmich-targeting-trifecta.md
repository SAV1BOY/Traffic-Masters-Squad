# Targeting Trifecta (The 3 F's)

> **Author**: Nicholas Kusmich
> **Domain**: Audience Research and Targeting
> **Used by agents**: audience-researcher, media-buyer, strategy-orchestrator
> **Checklists**: audience-research-checklist, targeting-setup-checklist

## Overview

The Targeting Trifecta is Nicholas Kusmich's method for finding the top 4% of a market — the most qualified, most likely to buy prospects. It uses three research dimensions (Follow, Frequent, Fund) to build hyper-targeted audiences that go far beyond basic demographic or interest-based targeting. The result is smaller but dramatically more qualified audience pools.

## When to Use

- Building targeting for a new campaign or entering a new market
- When broad targeting is producing low-quality leads or poor ROAS
- Refining audiences after initial campaigns reveal who actually converts
- Research phase of campaign planning (before building ad sets)
- When the platform's default interest categories feel too generic

## The Framework

### F1: Follow (Who Do They Follow?)
1. **Influencers**: Which thought leaders, experts, or personalities does this audience follow?
2. **Brands**: Which brands does this audience engage with and admire?
3. **Media**: Which publications, podcasts, YouTube channels, or blogs do they consume?
4. **Communities**: Which online communities, Facebook groups, or forums are they active in?
5. **Research method**:
   - Survey existing customers: "Who do you follow in this space?"
   - Analyze competitor audiences and their followers
   - Use platform audience insights tools
   - Check social media "following" lists of ideal customers
6. **Targeting application**: Use influencer/brand names as interest targets, target followers of specific pages

### F2: Frequent (Where Do They Hang Out?)
1. **Websites**: Which websites do they visit regularly?
2. **Events**: Which conferences, meetups, or events do they attend?
3. **Physical locations**: Which stores, gyms, restaurants, or venues do they frequent?
4. **Online platforms**: Which apps, tools, or platforms do they use daily?
5. **Professional spaces**: Which associations, certifications, or organizations are they part of?
6. **Research method**:
   - Google Analytics affinity and in-market audiences
   - Customer interviews about daily routines and habits
   - Social listening for location and platform mentions
   - Competitor event attendee lists (public)
7. **Targeting application**: Use website categories, event-related interests, app usage, and location-based targeting

### F3: Fund (What Do They Already Spend Money On?)
1. **Products**: What products in adjacent categories do they already buy?
2. **Services**: What services or subscriptions are they paying for?
3. **Tools**: What software, tools, or equipment have they invested in?
4. **Education**: What courses, books, or training have they purchased?
5. **Lifestyle**: What does their spending pattern reveal about values and priorities?
6. **Research method**:
   - Customer surveys about current spending and tools used
   - Amazon reviews in related product categories
   - Credit card data insights (aggregated, privacy-compliant)
   - Forum discussions about purchases and recommendations
7. **Targeting application**: Target buyers of complementary products, subscribers to related services, purchasers of prerequisite items

### Building the Top 4% Audience
1. Map out 10-20 items for each F dimension
2. Identify overlaps: items that appear across multiple dimensions
3. Prioritize items where all three Fs converge (they follow it AND frequent it AND fund it)
4. Layer targeting: combine Follow + Frequent + Fund for hyper-precise ad sets
5. Start with the tightest overlaps, then expand to broader combinations

### From Research to Ad Sets
1. Create audience groups based on F combinations
2. Each group becomes a distinct ad set for testing
3. Highest-overlap groups get tested first (most likely to convert)
4. Track which F dimensions drive the best results
5. Use winner data to build lookalike audiences for scaling

## Key Concepts

- **Top 4%**: Not everyone in your market is ready to buy. The Trifecta finds the 4% who are most qualified.
- **Behavioral signals over demographics**: What someone follows, frequents, and funds reveals intent better than age or location.
- **Layered targeting**: Single-dimension targeting is weak. Combining Fs creates precision.
- **Fund is the strongest signal**: People who already spend money in related areas are the most likely buyers.
- **Research before targeting**: The Trifecta is a research framework first, a targeting framework second.

## Decision Rules

- IF you cannot name 10+ items for each F THEN your audience research is incomplete
- IF targeting is only based on demographics THEN apply the Trifecta to find behavioral signals
- IF ads are getting reach but no conversions THEN Fund-based targeting is likely missing (reaching non-buyers)
- IF CPA is excellent but audience is tiny THEN broaden from the tightest F-overlap to wider combinations
- IF a Follow-based audience outperforms THEN create lookalikes from that audience for scaling
- IF Fund-based targeting is unavailable on the platform THEN use Follow and Frequent as proxies

## Common Mistakes

- Relying on platform-suggested interests instead of doing original research
- Only using one F dimension (usually Follow) and ignoring the others
- Not validating research with actual customer data
- Making assumptions about what the audience follows/frequents/funds without evidence
- Creating audiences that are too narrow to exit the learning phase (minimum viable audience size)
- Forgetting that the Trifecta is for finding seed audiences — scaling requires broader expansion
