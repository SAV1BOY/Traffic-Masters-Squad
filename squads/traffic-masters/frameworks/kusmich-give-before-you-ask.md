# Give Before You Ask

> **Author**: Nicholas Kusmich
> **Domain**: Advertising Philosophy and Campaign Strategy
> **Used by agents**: strategy-orchestrator, copywriter, media-buyer
> **Checklists**: campaign-launch-checklist, ad-review-checklist

## Overview

Give Before You Ask is Nicholas Kusmich's foundational advertising philosophy. The core principle: provide genuine value to your audience before asking for anything in return. The best ads educate, entertain, or inspire — they do not interrupt with demands to buy. This philosophy informs every other Kusmich framework and fundamentally shapes how campaigns are structured, from targeting to creative to funnels.

## When to Use

- As the guiding principle for every campaign created
- When ads feel too "salesy" and performance is declining
- During creative review to evaluate whether ads provide value
- When cold audience campaigns consistently underperform
- Training new team members on campaign philosophy
- Client education on why value-first advertising outperforms direct pitches

## The Framework

### The Value Hierarchy
1. **Level 1 — Educate**: Teach something useful the audience did not know
   - How-to content, tips, frameworks, insights, data
   - Position the brand as a knowledgeable authority
   - Example: "3 mistakes most [audience] make with [problem]"

2. **Level 2 — Entertain**: Create content people enjoy consuming
   - Stories, humor, relatable situations, behind-the-scenes
   - Make the brand memorable and likeable
   - Example: Day-in-the-life, customer transformation stories

3. **Level 3 — Inspire**: Show what is possible
   - Case studies, success stories, vision-casting
   - Connect the audience's current pain to a better future
   - Example: Before/after transformations, milestone celebrations

### The Give-Ask Ratio
1. For cold audiences: 80% give, 20% ask (or even 100% give)
2. For warm audiences: 60% give, 40% ask
3. For hot audiences: 40% give, 60% ask
4. For existing customers: 50% give, 50% ask (maintain the relationship)
5. The ratio applies to both individual ads and overall campaign mix

### Applying to Campaign Elements

**Lead Magnets (The Ultimate Give)**
- The lead magnet IS the give — it must deliver genuine value
- It should solve a real problem, not just tease a solution
- Quality of lead magnet determines quality of leads
- Test: Would someone pay $20+ for this? If not, it is not good enough.

**Ad Copy (Value in Every Line)**
- Open with a valuable insight, not a pitch
- The ad itself should teach something, even if they never click
- Headlines should promise value: "How to..." / "The 3 keys to..." / "Why most..."
- Even CTAs should frame the action as receiving value, not spending money

**Targeting (Give to the Right People)**
- Value is only valuable if the recipient needs it
- Targeting ensures your generosity reaches people who will appreciate it
- Mismatched targeting wastes the value you are giving

**Funnel (Continue Giving After the Click)**
- Landing pages should deliver value before asking for information
- Email sequences should educate before pitching
- The entire customer journey should feel like receiving, not being sold

## Key Concepts

- **Reciprocity principle**: When you give value first, people feel naturally inclined to reciprocate
- **Trust before transaction**: Advertising is a relationship, not a one-night stand
- **Value as differentiation**: Most competitors lead with "buy now" — leading with value stands out
- **The overdelivery mindset**: Give more than expected at every touchpoint
- **Long-term compounding**: Value-first builds audiences that buy repeatedly, not just once

## Decision Rules

- IF an ad's primary message is "buy this" to a cold audience THEN rewrite with a value-first approach
- IF lead magnet conversion rate is below 20% THEN the perceived value is too low
- IF email unsubscribe rate is high THEN the give-ask ratio in nurture sequences is wrong
- IF cold traffic campaigns have high bounce rates THEN the landing page is asking before giving
- IF warm audience ROAS is strong but cold is weak THEN cold creative lacks sufficient value
- IF customer LTV is low THEN post-purchase give is insufficient — add more value to retention

## Common Mistakes

- Treating "give" as a gimmick rather than a genuine philosophy
- Creating low-quality lead magnets that damage trust instead of building it
- Giving once and then switching to constant asking (the ratio must persist)
- Confusing "giving value" with "giving discounts" — value is knowledge, insight, transformation
- Only giving in lead magnets but being pushy in ad copy (inconsistency)
- Thinking this approach is slower — it actually converts better at every stage
