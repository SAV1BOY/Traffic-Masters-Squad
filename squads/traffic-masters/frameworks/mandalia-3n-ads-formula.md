# 3N Ads Formula

> **Author**: Depesh Mandalia
> **Domain**: Ad Creative Development
> **Used by agents**: copywriter, creative-strategist, media-buyer
> **Checklists**: ad-creation-checklist, creative-review-checklist

## Overview

The 3N Ads Formula is Depesh Mandalia's proprietary system for creating ads that cut through noise and drive action. Every effective ad contains three elements: Narrative (the story that connects), Nuance (the specific detail that builds credibility), and Nudge (the action trigger). Ads missing any N become generic, forgettable, or unconvincing.

## When to Use

- Writing any new ad creative (copy, video scripts, image concepts)
- Reviewing existing ads that are underperforming
- Training creative teams on ad creation standards
- Brainstorming ad angles for a new product or offer
- Refreshing fatigued creative with new variations

## The Framework

### N1: Narrative (The Story/Angle That Connects)
1. Choose an angle that resonates with the target audience's reality
2. Types of narrative angles:
   - **Problem-agitation**: Start with a pain point they recognize
   - **Transformation**: Before/after story showing the journey
   - **Authority**: Expert or insider perspective that builds trust
   - **Contrarian**: Challenge a common belief in the market
   - **Social proof**: Real customer story with emotional weight
   - **Discovery**: "I found something most people don't know about..."
3. The narrative must feel authentic, not manufactured
4. One ad = one narrative. Never combine multiple stories.

### N2: Nuance (The Specific Detail That Makes It Credible)
1. Generic claims kill trust. Nuance adds believability.
2. Types of nuance:
   - **Specific numbers**: "37% improvement in 14 days" not "massive improvement"
   - **Sensory details**: Concrete descriptions that paint a picture
   - **Named elements**: Specific ingredients, methods, tools, processes
   - **Unexpected honesty**: Admitting a limitation builds trust
   - **Insider language**: Using terminology the audience recognizes
3. The nuance test: Could a competitor copy this exact claim? If yes, it lacks nuance.
4. Pull nuance from real data, real customer stories, real product details

### N3: Nudge (The Action Trigger)
1. The nudge converts interest into action
2. Types of nudges:
   - **Direct CTA**: Clear instruction on what to do next
   - **Urgency**: Time-limited opportunity (must be genuine)
   - **Scarcity**: Limited availability (must be real)
   - **Risk reversal**: Guarantee that removes the downside
   - **Social momentum**: "Join 10,000+ others who already..."
   - **Curiosity gap**: "Click to see how this works for [their situation]"
3. Match nudge intensity to audience temperature:
   - Cold = soft nudge (learn more, see how)
   - Warm = medium nudge (get your free guide, watch the demo)
   - Hot = direct nudge (buy now, claim your spot, start today)

### Assembly Process
1. Start with N1 — choose the narrative angle based on avatar insights
2. Layer in N2 — add specific, credible details throughout
3. Close with N3 — match the nudge to the audience temperature and funnel stage
4. Review: Does the ad flow naturally from story to detail to action?

## Key Concepts

- **All 3 Ns are required**: Narrative without Nuance is vague. Nuance without Narrative is dry. Both without Nudge waste the attention you earned.
- **Narrative is the hook**: It earns the right to be read or watched
- **Nuance is the proof**: It makes the narrative believable
- **Nudge is the close**: It converts attention into action
- **One ad, one angle**: Do not try to cram multiple narratives into a single ad

## Decision Rules

- IF CTR is low THEN N1 (Narrative) is weak — the angle does not connect
- IF CTR is high but conversion is low THEN N2 (Nuance) lacks credibility or N3 (Nudge) is mismatched
- IF engagement is high (likes, comments, shares) but clicks are low THEN N3 (Nudge) is missing or too weak
- IF the ad feels generic THEN N2 (Nuance) is absent — add specific details
- IF the ad feels pushy THEN N3 (Nudge) is too aggressive for the audience temperature

## Common Mistakes

- Writing ads with no clear narrative — just listing features and benefits
- Using vague claims that any competitor could make (no nuance)
- Mismatching nudge intensity to audience temperature (hard sell to cold traffic)
- Forcing all three Ns into the first two lines instead of letting them flow naturally
- Copying competitor ads, which strips away the nuance that made them work
- Neglecting to test multiple narrative angles for the same product
