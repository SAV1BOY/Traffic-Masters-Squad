# 3 Acts Cinematic Storytelling Framework
> **Author**: Tom Breeze / Video Ad Narrative
> **Domain**: YouTube Ads, Video Scripts, Storytelling
> **Used by agents**: creative-strategist-agent, video-script-agent, youtube-ads-agent

## Overview
A cinematic storytelling structure for video ads adapted from screenwriting. Divides a
60-90 second ad into three acts: Setup, Confrontation, and Resolution. Each act has a
specific job — hook and contextualize, deepen and prove, then resolve and convert. Creates
narrative tension that holds attention through to the CTA.

## When to Use
- Creating story-driven YouTube or social video ads (60-90 seconds)
- When educational or direct-response formats feel stale
- Brand campaigns that need emotional engagement plus conversion
- Video testimonials or case study ads that need narrative structure

## The Framework
### Act I — Setup (0-15 seconds)
- **Hook (0-5s)**: Pattern interrupt or provocative opening statement
- **Context (5-10s)**: Set the scene — who, where, what situation
- **Problem (10-15s)**: Introduce the core conflict or pain point
- Goal: Viewer understands the world and feels the tension

### Act II — Confrontation (15-60 seconds)
- **Deepen (15-25s)**: Explore the problem's consequences and emotional weight
- **Solution (25-40s)**: Introduce the turning point — the product or method
- **Credibility (40-60s)**: Prove the solution works with evidence and story
- Goal: Viewer believes transformation is real and possible for them

### Act III — Resolution (60-90 seconds)
- **CTA (60-70s)**: Clear call to action — specific next step
- **Urgency (70-80s)**: Why act now — scarcity, deadline, momentum
- **Future Pacing (80-90s)**: Paint the picture of life after taking action
- Goal: Viewer feels compelled and confident to act

## Key Concepts
- Each act must earn the viewer's attention for the next act
- Act I is about identification: "This is my story too"
- Act II is about belief: "This solution actually works"
- Act III is about action: "I need to do this now"
- Transition moments between acts should feel like natural story beats
- Pacing matters: Act I is fast, Act II is detailed, Act III is urgent

## Decision Rules
- IF ad is 60 seconds → compress Act II, keep Act I and III proportional
- IF ad is 90 seconds → full structure with extended Act II
- IF product is emotional (health, relationships) → emphasize Act I setup
- IF product is logical (B2B, SaaS) → emphasize Act II credibility
- IF audience is warm → shorten Act I, extend Act III with offer details

## Common Mistakes
- Act I too slow — losing viewers before the story begins
- Act II without proof — claims without evidence feel hollow
- Act III without urgency — viewers agree but do not act
- No emotional arc — presenting facts without narrative tension
- Making the brand the hero instead of the customer
- Forgetting that the viewer is the protagonist, not the product
