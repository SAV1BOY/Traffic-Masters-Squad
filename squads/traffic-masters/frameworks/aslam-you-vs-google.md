# You vs. Google Framework
> **Author**: Kasim Aslam / Google Ads Philosophy
> **Domain**: Google Ads, Platform Strategy, Advertiser Independence
> **Used by agents**: google-ads-agent, strategist-agent, audit-agent

## Overview
A foundational philosophy for Google Ads management: Google optimizes for Google's
revenue, not yours. Every default setting, recommendation, and automation is designed
to increase Google's earnings. Advertisers must question every suggestion, maintain
manual control where it matters, and prioritize their own data over Google's guidance.

## When to Use
- Setting up any new Google Ads account or campaign
- Reviewing Google's automated recommendations and optimization score
- Deciding whether to adopt new Google features or beta programs
- Training team members on critical thinking about platform defaults
- Whenever Google suggests changes that increase spend or reduce control

## The Framework
### Principle 1: Question Every Default
- Default campaign settings favor Google's revenue (broad match, maximize clicks)
- Review and manually set: match types, bidding, networks, locations, devices
- Opt out of Search Partners and Display Network by default
- Never accept auto-applied recommendations blindly

### Principle 2: Maintain Control
- Start with Manual CPC before any automated bidding
- Use exact and phrase match before broad match
- Set manual bid adjustments for devices, locations, schedules
- Control where your ads appear — use placement exclusions
- Own your conversion tracking — do not rely solely on Google's modeling

### Principle 3: Data Before Automation
- Gather 30-50 conversions before switching to automated bidding
- Let manual campaigns run long enough to establish baselines
- Use your own data to validate Google's reported conversions
- Cross-reference Google Ads data with GA4, CRM, and actual sales

### Principle 4: Protect Your Budget
- Set daily budgets conservatively during testing
- Use shared budgets cautiously — they can drain on underperformers
- Monitor search terms report weekly — add negatives aggressively
- Track actual ROAS in your system, not just Google's reported ROAS

## Key Concepts
- Google's optimization score is a sales tool, not a performance metric
- "Recommendations" are often ways to increase your spend on Google's terms
- Smart campaigns and auto-apply remove the controls you need most
- The advertiser who thinks critically outperforms the one who trusts blindly
- Your CRM data is the source of truth, not the Google Ads dashboard

## Decision Rules
- IF Google recommends broad match → test in a separate campaign first
- IF optimization score drops → review but do not auto-apply
- IF Google rep suggests changes → evaluate against your own data
- IF automated bidding underperforms → revert to manual CPC
- IF a new feature launches → wait 90 days for bugs to resolve

## Common Mistakes
- Blindly accepting Google's optimization suggestions
- Trusting Google's conversion modeling without cross-referencing
- Using Smart campaigns for anything other than very small local businesses
- Letting Google auto-apply recommendations without review
- Confusing Google's goals (revenue) with your goals (profit)
