# Question-Based Hook Formulas

> Question hooks create an open loop in the reader's mind, compelling them to seek the answer. Use these formulas to drive engagement across ads, emails, and content.

---

## Yes/No Qualifying Questions

These pre-qualify the audience by getting them to self-identify.

- "Are you spending more than $[amount]/month on ads without a clear ROI?"
- "Do you know your true cost per acquisition — or just your cost per click?"
- "Is your ad account actually profitable, or does it just look busy?"
- "Are you still boosting posts and hoping for the best?"
- "Have you ever paused a campaign because you couldn't tell if it was working?"
- "Is your landing page converting at least [X]%? If not, you're leaving money on the table."
- "Are you tracking the metrics that matter — or the ones that feel good?"
- "Do you know which 20% of your ad spend drives 80% of your results?"
- "Is your retargeting actually bringing people back — or just annoying them?"
- "Are you running ads to cold traffic without a warm-up strategy?"

---

## "What If" Possibility Questions

These invite the audience to imagine a better future.

- "What if you could cut your CPA in half without changing your budget?"
- "What if every dollar you spent on ads came back as three?"
- "What if you knew exactly which creative would win before you launched?"
- "What if your best month became your average month?"
- "What if scaling didn't mean sacrificing profitability?"
- "What if you had a system that told you what to optimize every morning?"
- "What if your next campaign outperformed everything you've ever run?"
- "What if onboarding a new channel took days instead of months?"
- "What if your team could focus on strategy instead of manual reporting?"
- "What if you never had to wonder 'is this working?' again?"

---

## "Why" Diagnostic Questions

These challenge assumptions and create curiosity about root causes.

- "Why do most Facebook ads fail in the first 72 hours?"
- "Why is your CPA rising even though your budget hasn't changed?"
- "Why do some brands scale to $1M/month while others plateau at $50K?"
- "Why did your best-performing ad suddenly stop working?"
- "Why are your competitors paying less for the same audience?"
- "Why does increasing budget sometimes decrease performance?"
- "Why do 90% of landing pages fail to convert above 3%?"
- "Why is your click-through rate high but your conversion rate low?"
- "Why do most media buyers ignore the metric that matters most?"
- "Why are you optimizing for the wrong stage of the funnel?"

---

## "How" Process Questions

These promise a method, process, or answer.

- "How do top DTC brands maintain a 5x ROAS at scale?"
- "How do you know when to kill an ad vs. give it more time?"
- "How are the best media buyers structuring campaigns in [year]?"
- "How do you turn a $10/day test budget into a scalable winner?"
- "How do you build an audience that actually converts?"
- "How can you predict ad fatigue before it kills your performance?"
- "How do you structure a campaign that the algorithm loves?"
- "How do you write ad copy that sells without sounding salesy?"
- "How do you allocate budget across channels when every platform claims credit?"
- "How do you build a testing framework that consistently finds winners?"

---

## Rhetorical / Challenge Questions

These challenge the reader's current approach and create productive discomfort.

- "If your current strategy is working, why are you reading this?"
- "When was the last time you audited your ad account from scratch?"
- "How much are you spending on ads you've never properly tested?"
- "If you can't explain your attribution model in one sentence, do you really have one?"
- "Your competitors are scaling right now — what are you waiting for?"
- "How long will you keep doing the same thing and expecting different results?"
- "You know your campaigns could perform better — so what's stopping you?"
- "If your agency disappeared tomorrow, could your team run your ads?"
- "What's the cost of another month of 'good enough' performance?"
- "When was the last time you saw a month-over-month improvement?"

---

## Contrarian / Myth-Busting Questions

These challenge conventional wisdom and spark engagement.

- "What if everything you've been told about audience targeting is wrong?"
- "Are lookalike audiences actually hurting your performance?"
- "Is your obsession with ROAS actually preventing you from scaling?"
- "What if broad targeting outperforms your carefully segmented audiences?"
- "Does creative really matter more than targeting? The data might surprise you."
- "Are you sabotaging your campaigns by optimizing too frequently?"
- "Is A/B testing actually slowing down your results?"
- "What if your 'winning' ad is actually leaving money on the table?"
- "Does higher ad spend always mean higher CPAs? Not necessarily."
- "Are you chasing the wrong benchmarks for your industry?"

---

## Specificity Questions (Number-Driven)

These use specific numbers to create concrete curiosity.

- "Can you name the 3 metrics that predict campaign failure before it happens?"
- "Do you know the 5 signals that tell you it's time to scale?"
- "What are the 7 elements every high-converting landing page has in common?"
- "Did you know 73% of ad spend is wasted on the wrong audience segments?"
- "Are you making any of these 4 budget allocation mistakes?"
- "Do you know the 3-second rule that determines if your video ad succeeds or fails?"
- "Can your campaigns pass this 10-point health check?"
- "Are you using all 6 creative formats that the algorithm rewards?"
- "Do you know the one metric that predicts long-term profitability?"
- "Have you checked these 5 settings that silently drain your budget?"

---

## Usage Guidelines

1. **Answer the question** — Every question hook must lead to a satisfying answer in the body copy; open loops without closure frustrate audiences
2. **One question per hook** — Stacking multiple questions dilutes impact
3. **Make it personal** — Use "you/your" to direct the question at the individual reader
4. **Test question vs. statement** — The same idea as a question often outperforms the statement version, but always test
5. **Avoid questions with obvious answers** — "Do you want more money?" is lazy; "Do you know which $3K of your $10K monthly spend is actually wasted?" is compelling
6. **Platform considerations** — Questions work exceptionally well in email subject lines, video ad openers, and social ad primary text; less effective in display ad headlines where space is limited
