# Transition Phrases for Video Scripts

> Smooth transitions keep viewers engaged throughout a video ad or script. Organized by transition type and position within the video.

---

## Hook to Problem

Transition from the opening hook into the problem statement.

- "Here's the thing..."
- "But here's what most people don't realize..."
- "Let me explain why this matters."
- "The reason I bring this up is..."
- "And if you've experienced this, you know exactly what I mean."
- "Sound familiar? You're not alone."
- "That's exactly the situation [audience] faces every day."
- "But behind those numbers is a bigger problem."
- "And it's more common than you think."
- "The truth is, this happens to nearly everyone in [industry]."

---

## Problem to Agitation

Transition from identifying the problem to amplifying its impact.

- "And it gets worse."
- "But that's just the surface — here's what's really happening underneath."
- "Now, you might think that's not a big deal. Let me show you why it is."
- "The longer this goes unchecked, the more it costs you."
- "And the ripple effect? It touches everything."
- "Here's where it really starts to hurt."
- "What most people don't see is the compounding effect."
- "Let me break down what that actually means for your bottom line."
- "And if you think this doesn't apply to you, consider this..."
- "Every day you ignore this, the gap between you and your competitors widens."

---

## Agitation to Solution

Transition from the pain to the introduction of the solution.

- "But there's good news."
- "Here's the part where things start to change."
- "So what's the fix? It's simpler than you think."
- "That's exactly why we built..."
- "Now, I'm going to show you how to fix this."
- "The solution isn't more effort — it's a different approach."
- "Fortunately, there's a proven way to solve this."
- "After years of dealing with this problem, we found the answer."
- "Which brings me to the breakthrough."
- "What if I told you there's a way to eliminate this problem entirely?"

---

## Solution to Proof

Transition from describing the solution to providing evidence.

- "But don't just take my word for it."
- "Let me show you the numbers."
- "Here's what happened when [client/brand] implemented this."
- "And the results speak for themselves."
- "Here's the proof."
- "Let me walk you through a real example."
- "We've seen this work over and over. Here's one case."
- "The data backs this up."
- "Here's what this looks like in practice."
- "And this isn't theory — these are real results from real accounts."

---

## Proof to CTA

Transition from evidence into the call to action.

- "So here's what you can do right now."
- "Ready to see these results for yourself?"
- "If this resonates with you, here's your next step."
- "Want to implement this in your own [business/account]?"
- "Here's how to get started."
- "The next move is yours."
- "All it takes to get started is..."
- "If you're serious about [desired outcome], here's what to do next."
- "Click below to..."
- "Don't wait — here's how you can take action today."

---

## Point to Point (Within Body)

Transition between key points in the middle of a script.

- "Now, let me show you the second piece of this."
- "But that's just step one. Here's step two."
- "The next thing you need to know is..."
- "And it gets even better."
- "Here's where it gets really interesting."
- "Now, there's another side to this."
- "Building on that..."
- "And there's one more thing that makes all the difference."
- "On top of that..."
- "Let me take this one step further."

---

## Objection Handling Transitions

Smoothly introduce and address anticipated objections.

- "Now, you might be thinking..."
- "I know what you're going to say..."
- "The most common pushback I get is..."
- "And if you're skeptical, I completely understand."
- "Here's the question everyone asks at this point."
- "You might be wondering if this works for [specific situation]."
- "I hear this all the time: '[Objection].' Here's the reality."
- "Let me address the elephant in the room."
- "Fair question. Let me answer that directly."
- "Before you dismiss this, consider..."

---

## Tone Shift Transitions

Shift from informational to emotional, or from casual to serious.

### Casual to Serious
- "Okay, let me get real with you for a second."
- "Jokes aside, this actually matters."
- "Let me be honest about something."
- "Here's where I need you to pay close attention."
- "I'm going to be direct with you."

### Serious to Hopeful
- "But here's the silver lining."
- "It doesn't have to be this way."
- "There's a way out of this cycle."
- "The good news? This is completely fixable."
- "But the story doesn't end there."

### Data to Story
- "Let me put that number in perspective."
- "Behind that stat is a real story."
- "Here's what those numbers actually look like in practice."
- "That percentage represents real people — here's one of them."
- "Numbers tell part of the story. Here's the rest."

### Story to Data
- "Now let me back that up with data."
- "And the numbers confirm what the story suggests."
- "That's not just an anecdote — here's the pattern across all our accounts."
- "And when we looked at the data, the pattern held."
- "Here's the hard evidence behind that experience."

---

## Recap / Summary Transitions

Transition into a summary or key takeaway.

- "So let me bring this all together."
- "Here's what it comes down to."
- "To recap the three things you need to remember..."
- "The bottom line is this."
- "If you take away one thing from this, let it be..."
- "Let me summarize what we've covered."
- "Here's the key insight from everything we just discussed."
- "In short..."
- "The big picture? It's simple."
- "Everything I've shown you today points to one conclusion."

---

## Usage Guidelines

1. **Transitions should be invisible** — The best transition doesn't feel like a transition; it feels like a natural continuation
2. **Match tone to platform** — YouTube allows longer, more narrative transitions; TikTok/Reels need faster pivots
3. **Use verbal and visual transitions** — Pair the spoken transition with a visual change (cut, graphic, B-roll) for maximum effect
4. **Don't over-transition** — Not every sentence needs a bridge; sometimes a direct cut is more effective
5. **Practice out loud** — Transitions that read well often sound stiff when spoken; test them verbally
6. **Time your transitions** — In a 60-second ad, each section gets roughly 10-15 seconds; transitions should take 2-3 seconds max
