# Apple: Think Different Campaign

## Campaign Overview

**Brand:** Apple Computer, Inc.
**Launch Date:** September 28, 1997
**Campaign Duration:** 1997-2002
**Agency:** TBWA\Chiat\Day (Lee Clow, Creative Director)
**Primary Channels:** Television, print, outdoor
**Narrator:** Richard Dreyfuss (TV spot)
**Result:** Transformed Apple's brand perception and set the foundation for the most valuable company in history

---

## Strategic Context

### The Crisis
When Steve Jobs returned to Apple in 1997, the company was 90 days from bankruptcy. Market share had fallen below 4%. The product line was bloated and unfocused. Morale was destroyed. Apple needed a reason to exist beyond product specifications.

### Competitive Landscape
- Microsoft Windows dominated with 90%+ market share
- Dell and Compaq were winning on price
- Apple's products were not yet differentiated enough to sell on features alone
- The "Mac vs. PC" war was being fought on specs, a battle Apple could not win

### The Strategic Insight
Jobs and Lee Clow recognized that Apple could not outspend or out-spec the competition. Instead, they would redefine the competition. Apple would not sell computers. Apple would sell a worldview. The brand would stand for creativity, rebellion, and the people who push humanity forward.

---

## Creative Analysis

### The Television Spot

**Format:** 60-second brand anthem
**Footage:** Black-and-white archival footage of iconic figures
**Narration:** Richard Dreyfuss reading Jobs' manifesto

**Featured Figures:**
Albert Einstein, Bob Dylan, Martin Luther King Jr., John Lennon, Muhammad Ali, Ted Turner, Maria Callas, Mahatma Gandhi, Amelia Earhart, Alfred Hitchcock, Martha Graham, Jim Henson, Frank Lloyd Wright, Pablo Picasso, and others.

### The Copy

> "Here's to the crazy ones. The misfits. The rebels. The troublemakers. The round pegs in the square holes. The ones who see things differently. They're not fond of rules. And they have no respect for the status quo. You can quote them, disagree with them, glorify or vilify them. About the only thing you can't do is ignore them. Because they change things. They push the human race forward. And while some may see them as the crazy ones, we see genius. Because the people who are crazy enough to think they can change the world, are the ones who do."

### Creative Principles at Work

**1. Association Transfer**
By placing Apple alongside Einstein, Lennon, and King, the campaign transferred the attributes of genius and rebellion to the brand. Apple did not claim to be innovative; it stood next to innovators and let the association do the work.

**2. Aspirational Identity**
The ad did not describe a customer. It described who the customer wanted to be. Buying Apple became an identity statement: "I am one of the crazy ones."

**3. Absence of Product**
No computer was shown. No features were listed. No price was mentioned. The campaign sold belonging to a tribe, not a machine. This is the purest expression of brand advertising.

**4. Emotional Resonance Over Rational Argument**
The campaign made people feel something. It created an emotional response that no spec sheet could match. This emotional foundation would later make every product launch more effective.

## Media Strategy

### Television
- Premiered during the series premiere of ABC's "Wonderful World of Disney"
- Aired during primetime on major networks
- 60-second format gave the message room to build emotional momentum

### Print
- Full-page black-and-white portraits in major publications
- Each featured a single iconic figure with the Apple logo and "Think Different" tagline
- Ran in The Wall Street Journal, Newsweek, Time, and tech publications

### Outdoor
- Large-format billboards in major cities
- Iconic portraits became cultural artifacts in urban landscapes
- Subway and bus shelter placements in key markets

### Internal Marketing
- Every Apple employee received "Think Different" posters
- The campaign was as much an internal rallying cry as an external message
- Jobs used it to rebuild company culture and attract talent

## Results and Impact

### Immediate Business Impact
- Apple's stock price rose 3x within a year of the campaign launch
- Brand perception shifted dramatically in tracking studies
- The iMac (launched 1998) benefited from the brand halo created by Think Different
- Employee morale and retention improved significantly

### Long-Term Brand Value
- Established the emotional brand foundation that supports Apple's premium pricing to this day
- Created the template for every Apple product launch: lead with emotion, follow with product
- Made Apple a lifestyle brand rather than a technology company
- The "Think Different" positioning evolved into "Shot on iPhone" and other user-centric campaigns

### Cultural Impact
- "Think Different" entered the cultural lexicon
- The campaign is studied in every major advertising and business school
- It demonstrated that brand advertising could directly drive business recovery
- Set the standard for purpose-driven brand campaigns for decades

## Paid Traffic Lessons

### 1. Brand Builds the Moat for Performance
Think Different created the brand equity that allowed every subsequent Apple product campaign to convert at extraordinary rates. Paid traffic managers who only run direct response are building on sand. Brand investment creates the conditions for performance efficiency.

### 2. Positioning Before Promotion
Apple did not run product ads when the brand was broken. They fixed the brand first, then launched products into a receptive market. When inheriting a damaged brand or entering a new market, invest in positioning before scaling acquisition campaigns.

### 3. Simplicity Scales
"Think Different." Two words. The simpler the message, the more it can scale across channels, formats, and audiences. Complex value propositions fragment as they scale. Simple ones compound.

### 4. Sell the Identity, Not the Product
The most effective ads do not sell what the product does; they sell who the customer becomes. This applies directly to modern paid social, where creative that reflects the audience's aspirational identity consistently outperforms feature-focused ads.

### 5. Internal Alignment Amplifies External Messaging
Think Different worked because every Apple employee believed it. When running campaigns for clients, ensure the internal team is aligned with the messaging. Dissonance between external advertising and customer experience destroys ROAS.

### 6. Emotion Creates Memory Structures
Byron Sharp's research confirms what Think Different demonstrated: brands grow by building mental availability. Emotional advertising creates stronger, more durable memory structures than rational advertising. This affects everything from ad recall to click-through rates.

## Application to Modern Paid Traffic

### Brand Campaign Structure
When launching or relaunching a brand on paid channels:
1. **Phase 1 (Weeks 1-4):** Run brand anthem content optimized for reach and video views
2. **Phase 2 (Weeks 3-6):** Layer in product-specific content that inherits the brand positioning
3. **Phase 3 (Weeks 5+):** Launch direct response campaigns with brand-consistent creative
4. **Measure:** Track branded search volume, direct traffic, and organic social mentions alongside CPA

### Creative Testing Framework
- Test identity-based hooks against feature-based hooks
- Test aspirational messaging against problem-solution messaging
- Measure not just immediate conversion but downstream metrics (LTV, repeat purchase, referral)
