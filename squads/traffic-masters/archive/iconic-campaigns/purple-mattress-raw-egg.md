# Purple Mattress: The Raw Egg Test

## Campaign Overview

**Brand:** Purple Mattress
**Launch Date:** 2016 (original Kickstarter); scaled YouTube campaign 2016-2018
**Campaign Type:** Product demonstration video ad (long-form YouTube)
**Primary Channel:** YouTube (pre-roll + discovery), Facebook
**Agency/Creator:** Harmon Brothers (Provo, Utah)
**Budget:** ~$500,000 production; scaled to multi-million dollar media spend
**Result:** $75M+ in directly attributable revenue from the video; Purple became the fastest-growing mattress brand in the U.S.

---

## Strategic Context

### Market Landscape
- DTC mattress category was already crowded (Casper, Leesa, Tuft & Needle)
- Most DTC mattress brands competed on lifestyle messaging, unboxing experiences, and free trials
- Technical differentiation was difficult to communicate (every brand claimed "best sleep")
- Customer acquisition costs in the mattress category were rising rapidly
- YouTube was underutilized by mattress brands relative to Facebook and Google Search

### The Product Challenge
Purple's Hyper-Elastic Polymer grid was genuinely different from memory foam competitors, but explaining a novel material through standard advertising was difficult. The product needed demonstration, not description.

### Strategic Insight
The Harmon Brothers identified that Purple's material properties could be demonstrated in a visually compelling and shareable way. Instead of claiming superiority, they would prove it with an undeniable physical demonstration.

## Creative Breakdown

### The Video: "The Raw Egg Test"

**Format:** 3 minutes 48 seconds (long-form)
**Concept:** A Goldilocks-themed character drops raw eggs from a height onto a sheet of glass placed on a Purple mattress. The eggs survive. On a memory foam competitor, they break.

### Creative Structure

**Hook (0-10 seconds)**
- Opens with the promise of a dramatic demonstration
- Goldilocks character establishes humorous tone immediately
- The viewer is told they will see something they have not seen before

**Problem Establishment (10-45 seconds)**
- Explains why mattresses matter (pressure points, pain, poor sleep)
- Uses humor and relatable scenarios to keep attention
- Establishes that most mattress brands are "all the same"

**The Demonstration (45 seconds - 2 minutes)**
- Raw eggs placed on glass on the Purple mattress
- Weight dropped onto the eggs
- Eggs survive, proving the pressure-distributing properties
- Same test on a memory foam mattress: eggs break
- The demonstration is simple, visual, and undeniable

**Product Explanation (2-3 minutes)**
- Explains the Hyper-Elastic Polymer technology
- Shows the grid structure and how it distributes pressure
- Connects the demonstration to sleep quality

**Call to Action (3-3:48)**
- Direct response CTA with specific offer
- URL and phone number
- Urgency elements (limited-time pricing)

### Why the Demonstration Format Works

**1. Seeing Is Believing**
In a market full of subjective claims ("best sleep ever"), an objective demonstration cuts through. The raw egg test is binary: eggs survive or they don't. This removes the need for the viewer to trust the brand's claims.

**2. Visual Memorability**
The image of eggs surviving a drop test is unusual, surprising, and memorable. It creates a "mental bookmark" that persists long after viewing. When the viewer later considers a mattress purchase, the Purple egg test is recalled.

**3. Shareability Through Spectacle**
The demonstration has inherent "did you see this?" energy. Viewers share it not as an ad but as an interesting video, reducing the psychological resistance to sharing branded content.

**4. Long-Form Justifies Price**
At nearly 4 minutes, the video provides enough information to justify a considered purchase. Mattresses are high-consideration purchases; short-form ads struggle to move viewers to action. The long format matches the decision complexity.

## YouTube Strategy

### Targeting Approach

**1. In-Market Audiences**
- Targeted users actively researching mattresses on Google/YouTube
- In-market segments for bedding, home furnishing, and sleep products
- Custom intent audiences based on competitor brand searches

**2. Competitor Conquesting**
- Targeted viewers of competitor mattress review videos
- Pre-roll placement on mattress comparison content
- Discovery ads appearing alongside mattress-related searches

**3. Interest-Based Prospecting**
- Broader targeting to home owners, new movers, newly married
- Health and wellness interest categories
- Age-based targeting aligned with mattress purchase cycles

### Ad Format Strategy

| Format | Length | Objective | Placement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full video | 3:48 | Conversion | YouTube Discovery, In-Stream (skippable) |
| :30 cut-down | 0:30 | Awareness + traffic | YouTube Pre-roll (non-skippable) |
| :15 bumper | 0:15 | Retargeting/reminder | YouTube Bumper |
| :60 edit | 1:00 | Consideration | Facebook/Instagram In-Feed |

### Bidding and Optimization
- Initially optimized for views (maximize distribution of the demonstration)
- Transitioned to target CPA bidding as conversion data accumulated
- Used view-through conversion tracking to capture delayed mattress purchases
- Maintained a 30-day attribution window given the long consideration cycle

### Scaling on YouTube

**Phase 1: Validation (Weeks 1-4)**
- $5,000-$10,000/day budget
- Tested across 5-8 audience segments
- Identified winning audience/placement combinations
- Established baseline CPA and view-through rates

**Phase 2: Controlled Scale (Weeks 4-12)**
- Increased to $25,000-$50,000/day
- Expanded to broader audiences while monitoring CPA
- Introduced cut-down versions for different funnel stages
- Built retargeting pools from video viewers

**Phase 3: Full Scale (Months 3-12)**
- $100,000+/day across YouTube and Facebook
- Produced sequel videos maintaining the demonstration format
- Expanded to new creative concepts (Sasquatch test, real customer reactions)
- International expansion with localized versions

## Performance Metrics

### Direct Attribution
- Over 300 million views across platforms
- $75M+ in directly attributable revenue from the original video
- Estimated 10:1+ ROAS on the original creative
- The video remained the primary acquisition creative for 18+ months

### Business Impact
- Purple grew from a Kickstarter project to over $400M in annual revenue within 3 years
- Went public (NYSE: PRPL) in 2018
- The raw egg test became the defining brand asset
- Customer acquisition cost was significantly lower than industry average during peak performance

### YouTube-Specific Metrics
- Average view duration: approximately 2 minutes 30 seconds (66% completion for a 3:48 video)
- View-through rate on skippable placements: 35%+ (industry average ~15-20%)
- Click-through rate: 2.5-4% (significantly above category benchmark)

## Paid Traffic Lessons

### 1. Demonstration Beats Declaration
Showing beats telling. In any category where product performance can be visually demonstrated, a demonstration ad will outperform a claims-based ad. This applies to software (screen recordings), physical products (stress tests), services (before/after), and tools (live use).

**Application:** Audit your product for demonstrable features. What can you show that competitors cannot? Build creative around that proof point.

### 2. Long-Form Works When Content Earns Attention
The advertising industry's obsession with short-form is misguided for high-consideration purchases. The raw egg test video held attention for nearly 4 minutes because every second earned the next second. Length is not the enemy; boredom is.

**Application:** Test long-form (2-5 minute) video ads on YouTube for products with purchase prices above $100. Optimize for engaged views, not just impressions.

### 3. YouTube Is a Conversion Channel, Not Just an Awareness Channel
Purple proved that YouTube could drive direct response at scale. The combination of intent-based targeting, long-form creative, and conversion optimization made YouTube a primary revenue driver.

**Application:** Allocate budget to YouTube with direct response goals. Use TrueView for Action campaigns with target CPA bidding. Build custom intent audiences from competitor search terms.

### 4. One Hero Creative Can Carry a Brand
The raw egg test video was Purple's primary creative asset for over 18 months. In an industry obsessed with constant creative refresh, Purple proved that a truly exceptional piece of creative can sustain performance far longer than conventional wisdom suggests.

**Application:** Invest more in fewer, higher-quality creative concepts rather than producing high volumes of mediocre ads. A single great video is worth more than 50 average ones.

### 5. Humor Extends Watch Time and Reduces Skip Rates
The Goldilocks character and comedic tone were not frivolous. They served a strategic purpose: keeping viewers watching through the demonstration and CTA. Humor is a retention tool.

**Application:** Even in serious categories, test humor-infused creative. Track skip rates and average view duration as key metrics alongside conversion.

### 6. The Harmon Brothers Model: Invest in Production
The $500,000 production budget was unusual for a DTC brand, but the ROI justified it. The quality of the production, writing, and performance elevated the creative above the sea of low-budget DTC ads.

**Application:** Calculate the potential ROI of a high-production creative by estimating its lifespan and revenue potential. A $100,000 video that generates $5M in revenue over 12 months is far more efficient than twelve $10,000 videos that generate $200K each.

## Demonstration Ad Framework

### For Products with Demonstrable Differentiation

1. **Identify the proof point:** What can your product do that competitors cannot?
2. **Design the test:** Create a simple, visual, binary test (pass/fail, before/after)
3. **Add drama:** Make the stakes of the test visually compelling (raw eggs, not ping pong balls)
4. **Include comparison:** Show the competitor failing the same test (carefully, to avoid legal issues)
5. **Wrap in entertainment:** Use humor, character, or storytelling to make the demonstration watchable
6. **End with direct response:** Include clear CTA, URL, offer, and urgency
7. **Produce at quality:** Invest in production value commensurate with the revenue potential
8. **Scale on YouTube first:** Use YouTube's intent-based targeting and long-form format capabilities
