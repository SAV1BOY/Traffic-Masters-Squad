# Glossary of Terms

> Complete glossary of terms used throughout the Traffic Masters Squad system.

---

## A

**A/B Test** — An experiment comparing two variants (A and B) of a single variable to determine which performs better against a defined metric.

**Ad Fatigue** — The decline in ad performance caused by audience overexposure. Characterized by declining CTR and rising CPA as frequency increases.

**Ad Relevance Score** — A platform-assigned quality score reflecting how well an ad matches the interests of the target audience. Called "Ad Relevance Diagnostics" on Meta and "Quality Score" on Google.

**Ad Set** — A grouping within a campaign that defines the audience, budget, schedule, and placements. Called "Ad Group" on Google Ads.

**AOV (Average Order Value)** — The average dollar amount spent each time a customer places an order. Calculated as total revenue divided by number of orders.

**Attribution** — The process of assigning credit for a conversion to the touchpoints (ads, clicks, views) that contributed to it.

**Attribution Window** — The time period after an ad interaction during which a conversion can be attributed to that ad. Example: "7-day click, 1-day view."

**Audience Exclusion** — Removing a specific audience segment from seeing ads. Used to prevent serving ads to existing customers, recent converters, or irrelevant users.

**Audience Overlap** — The percentage of users shared between two or more audience segments. High overlap causes campaigns to compete against each other.

---

## B

**Bid Cap** — A maximum amount you're willing to pay per result in the auction. The platform will not bid above this amount.

**Bid Strategy** — The approach used to set bids in the ad auction. Options include automatic, target CPA, target ROAS, manual, cost cap, and bid cap.

**Blended Metrics** — Performance metrics calculated across all campaigns, ad sets, or channels combined, rather than individually.

**BOFU (Bottom of Funnel)** — The decision stage of the buyer journey where prospects are ready to convert. Targets high-intent audiences with direct-response offers.

**Bounce Rate** — The percentage of visitors who leave a landing page without taking any action. High bounce rates may indicate poor message match or page quality.

**Broad Targeting** — Targeting with minimal restrictions, allowing the platform algorithm to find the best audience. Also called "open targeting."

---

## C

**CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)** — The total cost to acquire a new customer, including all marketing and sales expenses. Different from CPA, which typically measures a single conversion event.

**Campaign** — The top-level organizational unit in an ad platform. Contains ad sets/groups and ads, and defines the objective and budget type.

**CAPI (Conversions API)** — Server-side tracking that sends conversion data directly from the advertiser's server to the ad platform, bypassing browser limitations and ad blockers.

**CBO (Campaign Budget Optimization)** — A setting where the platform distributes budget across ad sets within a campaign based on performance. Called "Advantage Campaign Budget" on Meta.

**Cohort** — A group of users who share a common characteristic or experience within a defined time period. Used in cohort analysis to compare behavior over time.

**Conversion** — A desired action taken by a user, such as a purchase, lead form submission, sign-up, or download.

**Conversion Rate (CVR)** — The percentage of users who complete a conversion out of those who clicked or visited. Calculated as conversions divided by clicks (or sessions).

**Cost Cap** — A bid strategy that tells the platform to get the lowest cost per result while trying to stay at or below a target cost.

**CPA (Cost Per Acquisition)** — The average cost to achieve one conversion. Calculated as total spend divided by total conversions.

**CPC (Cost Per Click)** — The average cost of a single click on an ad. Calculated as total spend divided by total clicks.

**CPL (Cost Per Lead)** — The average cost to generate one lead. A variant of CPA specific to lead generation objectives.

**CPM (Cost Per Mille)** — The cost per 1,000 impressions. A measure of how much it costs to reach 1,000 people.

**Creative** — The visual and textual content of an ad, including images, videos, headlines, body copy, and CTAs.

**CRM (Customer Relationship Management)** — A system for managing customer data and interactions. CRM data is often used for audience targeting and exclusion.

**Cross-Domain Tracking** — Tracking user journeys across multiple website domains while maintaining a single session.

**CTA (Call to Action)** — The specific action an ad asks the viewer to take. Examples: "Learn More," "Shop Now," "Sign Up."

**CTR (Click-Through Rate)** — The percentage of people who click an ad after seeing it. Calculated as clicks divided by impressions.

---

## D

**Dayparting** — Scheduling ads to run only during specific hours of the day or days of the week.

**DTC (Direct-to-Consumer)** — A business model where brands sell directly to consumers without intermediaries. Common in e-commerce.

**Dynamic Creative** — An ad feature that automatically tests different combinations of creative elements (images, headlines, descriptions) to find the best-performing combination.

---

## F

**First-Party Data** — Data collected directly by a business from its own customers and website visitors. Considered more reliable and privacy-compliant than third-party data.

**Frequency** — The average number of times each person in your audience has seen your ad. High frequency can indicate ad fatigue.

**Frequency Cap** — A limit on how many times a single person can see your ad within a defined period.

**Full-Funnel** — A campaign strategy that addresses all stages of the buyer journey, from awareness through conversion and retention.

**Funnel Stage** — A position within the buyer journey: TOFU (awareness), MOFU (consideration), or BOFU (decision/conversion).

---

## G

**GA4 (Google Analytics 4)** — Google's analytics platform for measuring website and app activity.

**GTM (Google Tag Manager)** — A tag management system for deploying and managing tracking tags on websites without code changes.

---

## I

**Impression** — A single instance of an ad being displayed to a user.

**Incrementality** — The additional conversions that would not have occurred without advertising. Measures the true causal impact of ads.

---

## K

**KPI (Key Performance Indicator)** — A measurable value that indicates how effectively a campaign is achieving its objective. Primary KPIs drive decisions; secondary KPIs provide context.

---

## L

**Landing Page** — The web page a user arrives at after clicking an ad. Optimized for conversion toward the campaign objective.

**Lead** — A prospect who has provided contact information or expressed interest in a product/service.

**Learning Phase** — The initial period after campaign launch or significant change where the platform algorithm is calibrating delivery. Performance may be volatile during this phase. Typically requires 50 conversions to complete (Meta).

**Lifetime Budget** — A budget type where the total budget is set for the entire duration of the campaign, and the platform paces daily spend.

**Lookalike Audience** — An audience built by a platform to find users similar to a source audience (e.g., existing customers). Defined by percentage (1% = most similar).

**LTV (Lifetime Value)** — The total revenue a customer generates over their entire relationship with a business.

---

## M

**Marginal CPA** — The cost of acquiring one additional conversion at a given spend level. Used to identify optimal budget levels.

**Message Match** — The consistency between ad messaging and landing page messaging. Strong message match improves conversion rates.

**MOFU (Middle of Funnel)** — The consideration stage of the buyer journey where prospects are evaluating solutions. Targets warm audiences with educational and comparative content.

**MTA (Multi-Touch Attribution)** — An attribution model that distributes conversion credit across multiple touchpoints in the customer journey.

---

## P

**Pacing** — The rate at which a campaign spends its budget relative to the planned schedule. On-pace means spending as expected.

**Pixel** — A snippet of code placed on a website that tracks user actions and sends data to an ad platform for conversion tracking and optimization.

**Placement** — Where an ad appears within a platform's ecosystem (e.g., Facebook Feed, Instagram Stories, Google Search, YouTube pre-roll).

---

## Q

**Quality Gate** — A checkpoint within a workflow that must be passed before work advances. Used to ensure consistency, accuracy, and compliance.

**Quality Score** — Google Ads' rating of the quality and relevance of keywords, ads, and landing pages. Affects ad rank and CPC.

---

## R

**Reach** — The total number of unique people who see an ad, as opposed to impressions (which count repeat views).

**Registry** — A structured data record tracking campaigns, creatives, audiences, experiments, and other entities. The single source of truth.

**Retargeting** — Showing ads to people who have previously interacted with your brand (visited website, watched video, engaged with content).

**ROAS (Return on Ad Spend)** — Revenue generated per dollar of ad spend. Calculated as revenue divided by ad spend. A ROAS of 3.0 means $3 in revenue for every $1 spent.

---

## S

**Scaling** — The process of increasing campaign spend while maintaining or improving efficiency. Can be vertical (more budget) or horizontal (new audiences/channels).

**Seed Audience** — The source audience used to create a lookalike audience. Typically composed of best customers or high-value converters.

**Statistical Significance** — The probability that test results are not due to random chance. Typically requires 95% confidence to declare a test winner.

---

## T

**TOFU (Top of Funnel)** — The awareness stage of the buyer journey. Targets cold audiences who are unfamiliar with the brand, using broad reach and educational content.

**Tracking** — The system of tools and tags used to measure user actions and attribute them to advertising touchpoints.

---

## U

**UGC (User-Generated Content)** — Content created by users/customers rather than the brand. Often used as ad creative for its authentic, relatable quality.

**UTM Parameters** — Tags added to URLs that enable analytics tools to identify the source, medium, campaign, content, and term associated with traffic.

---

## V

**View-Through Conversion** — A conversion attributed to an ad that was viewed but not clicked. The user saw the ad and later converted through another path.

---

## W

**Workflow** — A defined sequence of tasks, decision points, and quality gates that coordinate multiple agents to complete a process.
