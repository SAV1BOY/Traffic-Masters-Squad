# Political Advertising Rules

> Cross-platform reference for paid traffic professionals running political, election, and social issue advertising campaigns.

---

## 1. Overview

Political advertising is one of the most tightly regulated categories in digital marketing. Rules vary dramatically by platform, country, and election cycle. Non-compliance can result in immediate account suspension, legal liability, and public disclosure.

---

## 2. Platform-by-Platform Rules

### Meta (Facebook/Instagram)

**Authorization Required**:
- Advertisers must complete the Ad Authorization process before running political/social issue ads
- Requires government-issued ID verification and address confirmation
- Must confirm identity and location; takes 1-3 weeks to complete
- Each country has its own authorization process

**Ad Requirements**:
- "Paid for by" disclaimer is mandatory and visible on the ad
- Disclaimer must accurately identify the paying entity
- Ads are added to the Meta Ad Library and remain publicly visible for 7 years
- Must use the "Social Issues, Elections, or Politics" Special Ad Category

**Targeting Restrictions**:
- Special Ad Category targeting applies: limited demographic, interest, and behavioral targeting
- Cannot use Custom Audiences built from voter files in some jurisdictions
- Lookalike audiences replaced with "Special Ad Audiences"

**Content Rules**:
- No voter suppression content (e.g., "Don't bother voting, it doesn't matter")
- No content that could interfere with election processes
- No premature claims of election victory
- Deepfake/AI-generated content of political figures may be restricted

**Blackout Periods**:
- Meta may restrict new political ads in the final days before elections (has implemented 7-day freeze before US elections)
- Existing ads may continue but cannot be edited

**Ad Library**:
- All political/social issue ads stored in `facebook.com/ads/library`
- Includes spend ranges, impressions, demographic breakdowns
- Available to journalists, researchers, and the public
- API access available for bulk data analysis

### Google Ads (Search, Display, YouTube)

**Verification Required**:
- Must complete Google's advertiser identity verification
- Election ads require additional election advertising verification
- Must confirm identity, location, and (in some countries) the legal entity paying for the ads
- Verification timelines: 5-10 business days

**Ad Requirements**:
- "Paid for by" disclosure automatically appended to election ads
- Stored in Google's Ad Transparency Center
- In-ad disclosure must be clearly readable

**Targeting Restrictions**:
- **No microtargeting**: Cannot target political ads based on political affiliation, voting history, or political ideology
- Geographic, age, gender, and contextual targeting are allowed
- Remarketing to your own site visitors is allowed
- Customer Match lists allowed but cannot be based on political affiliation data alone

**Content Rules**:
- No deepfake or synthetically manipulated media that misrepresents candidates
- No demonstrably false claims about voting procedures (dates, locations, methods)
- No content designed to discourage voting

**Blackout Periods**:
- Google may implement election silence periods (has restricted ads after polls close in US elections)

### TikTok

**Political Ads: BANNED**
- TikTok prohibits all political advertising globally
- This includes: candidate ads, party ads, PAC/Super PAC ads, ballot measure ads, political fundraising
- Social issue advocacy is also restricted

**What IS Allowed**:
- Government agency awareness campaigns (public health, census)
- Nonprofit campaigns on social issues (with restrictions)
- News organization content promotion

**Enforcement**:
- TikTok actively removes political ads and may suspend accounts that attempt to run them
- "Advocacy ads" that are effectively political will be rejected

### LinkedIn

**Political Ads: BANNED (since 2018)**
- LinkedIn prohibits all political candidate and ballot measure advertising globally
- No election ads, no PAC ads, no political party ads

**What IS Allowed**:
- Government affairs/lobbying firms can advertise their services (not specific political positions)
- Policy think tanks can promote research (not advocate for specific candidates)
- Issue advocacy that does not reference elections, candidates, or ballot measures

### X (Twitter)

**Political Ads: Policy has evolved**
- Banned all political ads in 2019
- Partially lifted restrictions in 2023
- Current rules vary by country and election cycle
- Cause-based advertising allowed with restrictions in some markets
- Verify current policy before planning any campaign

### Snapchat

- Political ads allowed in some countries with verification
- Must register through Snap's political ads portal
- All political ads stored in Snap's Political Ads Library
- Geographic targeting minimum and restrictions apply
- Must comply with local election laws

### Programmatic/DSP

- No universal policy; each DSP has its own rules
- DV360: Follows Google's political ad policies
- The Trade Desk: Allows political ads with verification; state-level compliance required
- Amazon DSP: Political ads prohibited
- Transparency reports may be required depending on the DSP

## 3. Regulatory Framework by Region

### United States

**Federal Laws**:
- **FEC (Federal Election Commission)**: Requires "Paid for by" disclaimers on all federal candidate ads
- **Bipartisan Campaign Reform Act**: Regulates political ad spending and disclosure
- **Honest Ads Act** (proposed): Would extend broadcast disclosure rules to digital platforms; not yet law as of 2026
- **FCC rules**: Apply to broadcast/cable; do not directly govern digital ads

**State Laws (vary significantly)**:
- Many states require registration of political ad buyers
- Some states mandate digital ad disclosure/reporting (WA, CA, NY, NJ notable)
- Washington state: Requires detailed records of political ad purchases over $1,000
- California: Requires "Top Funders" disclosure for ballot measure ads

**Key Compliance Requirements**:
- All ads for federal candidates must include "Paid for by [Committee Name]"
- Coordinated expenditures with campaigns must be reported to FEC
- Independent expenditure reports required for ads exceeding $250
- Foreign nationals/entities prohibited from spending on US elections

### European Union

- **Digital Services Act (DSA)**: Requires transparency for all political ads
- **Proposed EU Political Advertising Regulation**: Would require clear labeling, targeting transparency, and cross-border coordination
- **Country-specific**: Each EU member state has additional election laws
- **Blackout periods**: Many EU countries prohibit political ads 24-48 hours before elections
- **France**: Prohibits all paid political advertising in the 6 months before an election
- **Germany**: Requires party/organization identification; media spending disclosures

### Brazil

- **TSE (Tribunal Superior Eleitoral)**: Regulates all election advertising
- **Paid political ads on social media**: Allowed only during official campaign period
- **Boosting**: Political content boosting allowed but must be from the candidate's official account
- **Reporting**: All digital ad spending must be reported to TSE
- **Penalties**: Non-compliance can result in candidate disqualification

### United Kingdom

- **Electoral Commission**: Regulates spending and disclosure
- **Imprint requirements**: All digital political ads must include the "imprint" (who paid for it)
- **Spending limits**: Apply during regulated periods before elections
- **Online Safety Act**: Additional transparency requirements for political content

## 4. Social Issue Advertising

Social issue ads (not directly about candidates but about political/social topics) face special treatment:

### Meta's Definition of Social Issues
Meta maintains a country-specific list. US examples include:
- Civil and social rights
- Crime
- Economy
- Education
- Environment
- Guns
- Health
- Immigration
- Political values and governance
- Security and foreign policy

**Key implication**: Even if your ad is not about a candidate, if it touches these topics, it must go through the Social Issues ad authorization process.

### When Issue Advocacy Becomes Political
If your ad:
- Mentions a candidate or elected official by name
- References a specific election, vote, or ballot measure
- Advocates for or against a specific policy with political implications
- Is timed to coincide with a legislative vote or election

...it may be classified as a political ad regardless of your intent.

## 5. Creative & Messaging Compliance

### Required Disclosures

| Element | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| **Paid for by** | Must identify the entity paying for the ad |
| **Authorized by** | If coordinated with a campaign, must identify the candidate |
| **Disclaimer placement** | Must be clearly visible; cannot be hidden or truncated |
| **Font size** | Must be legible; platform-specific minimums apply |

### Content Restrictions

**Universally prohibited**:
- False information about voting procedures (date, location, eligibility)
- Content designed to intimidate voters or suppress turnout
- Impersonation of election officials
- Deepfake content of candidates (emerging restriction across platforms)
- False claims about endorsements
- Premature victory declarations

**Generally restricted**:
- Manipulated or out-of-context media
- Inflammatory or inciting content
- Content that could trigger violence
- Foreign-sourced content targeting domestic elections

## 6. Operational Best Practices

### Pre-Campaign Checklist

- [ ] Complete advertiser verification on all platforms (start 3-4 weeks early)
- [ ] Register as a political advertiser where required
- [ ] Confirm compliance with state/local laws in all target geographies
- [ ] Set up proper "Paid for by" disclaimers on all creatives
- [ ] Brief creative team on prohibited content and claims
- [ ] Establish rapid-response process for ad disapprovals during election cycles
- [ ] Document all ad spending for regulatory reporting
- [ ] Set up Ad Library monitoring for competitive intelligence

### Timeline Planning

| Phase | Timing | Action |
|-------|--------|--------|
| **Verification** | 4-6 weeks before launch | Complete all platform verifications |
| **Creative development** | 3-4 weeks before | Develop compliant creative with proper disclaimers |
| **Testing** | 2-3 weeks before | Test ads for approval; fix any rejections |
| **Scaling** | Campaign period | Monitor daily for disapprovals or policy changes |
| **Blackout** | Final days before election | Prepare for potential ad restrictions |
| **Post-election** | After results | Pause or adjust messaging; update creative |

### Budget Considerations
- Higher CPMs during election seasons (political ads increase competition)
- US presidential election years: CPMs can increase 20-50% in swing states Q3-Q4
- Plan for 10-15% budget buffer for unexpected disapprovals/delays
- Front-load spending before potential blackout periods

## 7. Reporting & Transparency

### What Gets Disclosed Publicly

| Platform | Disclosed Information |
|----------|---------------------|
| **Meta Ad Library** | Creative, spend range, impressions, demographics, dates |
| **Google Transparency** | Creative, spend, impressions, targeting criteria |
| **Snap Political Ads** | Creative, spend, impressions, targeting |

### Record-Keeping Requirements
- Maintain records of all political ad purchases for at least 3-5 years
- Keep copies of all creative versions and approval records
- Document targeting criteria used
- Track spend by state/jurisdiction for regulatory reporting
- Retain correspondence with platforms about ad approvals/rejections

## 8. Common Mistakes & Consequences

| Mistake | Consequence | Prevention |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| Running political ads without verification | Immediate ad removal + potential account ban | Complete verification before launching |
| Missing "Paid for by" disclaimer | FEC fine + platform violation | Template all creative with disclaimers |
| Using voter file data for targeting on Meta | Policy violation + legal risk | Use platform-compliant targeting only |
| Running ads during blackout period | Ad removal + public disclosure | Calendar blackout dates; set campaigns to end early |
| Not reporting digital ad spending | FEC/state fines | Automate spend tracking and reporting |
| Foreign entity funding political ads | Federal criminal violation | Verify funding sources; document compliance |
