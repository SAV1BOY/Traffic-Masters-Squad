# Attention and Hooking Psychology
> **Category**: Psychology Applied to Advertising
> **Last Updated**: 2026-03

## The Attention Economy
- Average person sees 4,000-10,000 ads per day
- Social media scroll speed: 1-2 seconds per content piece
- Attention is the scarcest resource — earning it is the first job of any ad

## Hook Types That Capture Attention

### Pattern Interrupt
- Break the expected visual or auditory pattern in the feed
- Examples: unexpected color, unusual camera angle, jarring first frame
- Scroll-stopping images: faces with strong emotion, bright contrasts, unusual compositions

### Curiosity Gap
- Open a loop the brain needs to close
- Examples: "What happens when you...", "The one thing nobody tells you about..."
- Works best when the gap is narrow enough to seem answerable

### Controversy/Contrarian
- Challenge a commonly held belief
- Examples: "Stop running ads to cold traffic", "Your funnel is losing you money"
- Must be defensible — empty controversy backfires

### Direct Benefit
- Immediately state what the viewer will gain
- Examples: "How to cut your CPA by 40% in 7 days"
- Works best for solution-aware audiences

### Emotion Trigger
- Evoke a strong emotional response in the first moment
- Examples: fear, surprise, humor, empathy, outrage
- High-arousal emotions capture attention; low-arousal emotions do not

## Platform-Specific Hook Windows
- Facebook/Instagram feed: 1-3 seconds to hook
- TikTok: 0.5-1.5 seconds (fastest scroll speed)
- YouTube pre-roll: 5 seconds before skip button
- Google Search: headline is the only hook

## Hook Testing Framework
1. Create 3-5 hook variations per creative concept
2. Same body/offer, different hooks
3. Measure: hook rate (3-second video views / impressions), CTR
4. Winner: highest hook rate with acceptable downstream metrics

## Key Takeaways
- The hook is the most important element in any ad — invest disproportionate effort
- Test hooks separately from body content
- Video first 3 seconds and static headline/image are the hook surfaces
- Different audiences respond to different hook types — test all five
- A great offer with a bad hook will never be seen
