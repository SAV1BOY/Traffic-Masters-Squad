# Framing Effects in Ad Messaging

> How the same information presented differently produces dramatically different ad performance outcomes.

---

## 1. What Is the Framing Effect?

The framing effect occurs when people's decisions are influenced by how information is presented, rather than the information itself. Identical facts framed differently lead to different choices.

**Classic example**: "This surgery has a 90% survival rate" vs. "This surgery has a 10% mortality rate." Same data, dramatically different emotional responses.

---

## 2. Framing Types for Advertising

### Gain Frame vs. Loss Frame

| Gain Frame | Loss Frame |
|-----------|-----------|
| "Save $500 per year" | "Stop losing $500 per year" |
| "Get 20% more leads" | "You're missing 20% of your leads" |
| "Improve your health" | "Stop damaging your health" |
| "Grow your business" | "Don't let your business fall behind" |

**When to use each**:
- **Gain frame**: Low-risk decisions, aspirational products, positive brand associations
- **Loss frame**: High-stakes decisions, problem-aware audiences, urgency-driven offers

### Percentage vs. Absolute Numbers

| Scenario | Percentage Frame | Absolute Frame | More Persuasive |
|----------|-----------------|----------------|-----------------|
| Large base number | "Save 10%" | "Save $500" | Absolute ($500 sounds bigger) |
| Small base number | "Save 50%" | "Save $5" | Percentage (50% sounds bigger) |
| Risk/health | "3% risk" | "3 in 100 people" | Absolute (more vivid) |
| Discounts on cheap items | "50% off" | "$2.50 off" | Percentage |
| Discounts on expensive items | "10% off" | "$100 off" | Absolute |

**The Rule of 100** (Jonah Berger): For items under $100, use percentage. For items over $100, use absolute dollar amount. The larger-looking number feels like a better deal.

### Time Frame Reframing

| Original | Reframed | Effect |
|----------|----------|--------|
| "$365/year" | "$1/day" | Daily feels trivially small |
| "$99/month" | "$3.30/day" | "Less than your coffee" |
| "$2,400/year" | "$200/month" | Monthly is less shocking |
| "10 hours/week saved" | "520 hours/year saved" | Annual sounds massive |
| "2% improvement" | "2% compounded = 26% annually" | Compounding amplifies |

### Frequency Framing

| Less Persuasive | More Persuasive | Why |
|-----------------|-----------------|-----|
| "Floss daily" | "Floss tonight" | Specific > general |
| "Exercise more" | "Take a 10-minute walk now" | Concrete > abstract |
| "Use our tool regularly" | "Log in every Monday at 9 AM" | Scheduled > vague |

## 3. Framing in Ad Copy

### Headline Framing Examples

**Product: Project management software**

| Frame | Headline |
|-------|----------|
| Gain | "Finish projects 40% faster" |
| Loss | "Stop losing 40% of your productive time" |
| Social | "Join 10,000+ teams who deliver on time" |
| Curiosity | "Why the best teams never miss deadlines" |
| Authority | "The tool McKinsey uses to manage 500+ projects" |
| Comparison | "Twice the features at half the price of [Competitor]" |

### Body Copy Framing

**Positive/approach frame**:
"Our platform helps marketing teams create better campaigns, collaborate more effectively, and hit their targets consistently. Start your free trial and see the difference."

**Negative/avoidance frame**:
"Without the right tools, marketing teams waste hours on manual work, miss deadlines, and fall short of their goals. Don't let that be your team. Start your free trial today."

### CTA Framing

| Standard CTA | Reframed CTA | Frame Type |
|-------------|-------------|------------|
| "Sign Up" | "Start Growing" | Gain |
| "Buy Now" | "Get Yours" | Ownership |
| "Learn More" | "See How It Works" | Curiosity |
| "Subscribe" | "Join 10K+ Members" | Social |
| "Download" | "Get Your Free Copy" | Reciprocity |
| "Request Demo" | "See It In Action" | Curiosity |
| "Start Trial" | "Try It Risk-Free" | Loss aversion (risk removal) |

## 4. Price Framing Strategies

### Partitioned Pricing vs. All-Inclusive

| Approach | Example | Effect |
|----------|---------|--------|
| **Partitioned** | "$79 + $15 shipping" | Base price feels lower; total cost is obscured |
| **All-inclusive** | "$94 with free shipping" | "Free shipping" triggers reciprocity |
| **Drip pricing** | "$79" then fees added at checkout | Highest conversion but lowest trust; potential legal issues |

**Best practice**: All-inclusive with "free shipping" is the optimal frame for most e-commerce. Amazon trained consumers to expect it.

### Payment Framing

| Frame | Example | Best For |
|-------|---------|----------|
| Full price | "$997" | Anchoring the value |
| Installments | "3 payments of $349" | Making it accessible |
| Daily cost | "$3.30/day" | Making it seem trivial |
| Savings vs. cost | "Saves you $200/month" (not "costs $99/month") | Positive frame |
| Comparison | "Less than your Netflix subscription" | Relative value |

### Discount Framing

| Approach | When It Works Best |
|----------|-------------------|
| "Save $50" | High-price items (>$100) |
| "50% off" | Low-price items (<$100) |
| "Buy 2, Get 1 Free" | Products with high margins; increases AOV |
| "Free gift with purchase" | Luxury, beauty; perceived bonus value |
| "$100 value for $49" | Info products, bundles; anchoring frame |

## 5. Framing for Different Audiences

### Risk-Averse Audiences (Finance, Insurance, Health)

- Emphasize what they WON'T lose: "Protect your savings"
- Use loss frames: "Don't risk your family's security"
- Risk-removal language: "Guaranteed", "Insured", "Protected"
- Safety framing: "Trusted by 50,000+ families"

### Aspiration-Driven Audiences (Lifestyle, Fitness, Education)

- Emphasize what they WILL gain: "Transform your body"
- Use gain frames: "Unlock your potential"
- Identity framing: "Become the person you want to be"
- Future-state language: "Imagine your life in 90 days..."

### Logic-Driven Audiences (B2B, Tech, Finance Professionals)

- Use data framing: "47% improvement in pipeline velocity"
- ROI framing: "$4.20 return for every $1 invested"
- Comparison framing: Feature-by-feature vs. competitors
- Efficiency framing: "Cut your workflow from 8 steps to 2"

### Emotion-Driven Audiences (Consumer, Health, Parenting)

- Story framing: Customer narratives and transformations
- Community framing: "Join thousands who..."
- Impact framing: "Every purchase plants a tree"
- Relationship framing: "Made for moments that matter"

## 6. A/B Testing Framing

### High-Impact Framing Tests

| Test | Variant A | Variant B |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| Gain vs. Loss | "Get more leads" | "Stop losing leads" |
| % vs. Absolute | "Save 30%" | "Save $150" |
| Annual vs. Monthly | "$588/year" | "$49/month" |
| Feature vs. Benefit | "AI-powered analytics" | "Know exactly where to spend" |
| Problem vs. Solution | "Tired of manual reporting?" | "Automated reports in one click" |
| Social vs. Individual | "Join 5,000+ teams" | "Built for your team" |
| Rational vs. Emotional | "22% faster processing" | "Finally, software that respects your time" |

### Expected Performance Differences

Framing changes typically produce 5-30% differences in conversion rates. The largest gaps occur when:
- The audience has strong existing beliefs (confirmation framing)
- The decision is high-stakes (loss framing wins)
- The product is unfamiliar (social framing reduces uncertainty)
- The price is ambiguous (anchoring framing creates reference points)

## 7. Framing Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Example | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Negative frame creating anxiety | "Don't let your business fail" | Balance with a clear, achievable solution |
| Over-promising in gain frame | "10x your revenue guaranteed" | Use realistic, substantiable claims |
| Frame inconsistency across touchpoints | Ad says "save money", LP says "premium quality" | Align framing from ad to LP to checkout |
| Ignoring cultural context | Loss framing in cultures that value harmony | Research cultural framing preferences |
| Frame that doesn't match the product | Luxury product with discount framing | Match the frame to the positioning |
