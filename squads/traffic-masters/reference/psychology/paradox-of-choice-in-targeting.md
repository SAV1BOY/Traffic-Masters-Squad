# Paradox of Choice in Targeting
> **Category**: Psychology Applied to Advertising
> **Last Updated**: 2026-03

## Overview
The paradox of choice (Barry Schwartz) states that more options lead to decision paralysis, dissatisfaction, and lower action rates. This principle applies to both ad creative (what users see) and targeting strategy (how we build campaigns).

## Application to Ad Creative

### Landing Page Design
- Too many CTAs reduce conversion rate — use one primary CTA
- Too many product options on a landing page cause abandonment
- Curated selections ("Our top 3 picks") outperform full catalogs
- Progressive disclosure: show options in stages, not all at once

### Ad Copy
- One clear message per ad — do not try to say everything
- One offer per ad — multiple offers dilute each other
- One CTA per ad — "Buy now" or "Learn more", not both equally weighted
- Simplicity in value proposition: "We do X for Y" beats a feature list

### Offer Structure
- 3 pricing tiers is optimal (less causes suspicion, more causes paralysis)
- Highlight the recommended tier clearly
- Bundle complex features into simple packages
- Use comparison tables to simplify complex decisions

## Application to Targeting Strategy

### Audience Simplification
- Too many narrow ad sets create management complexity without proportional benefit
- Broad targeting + strong creative often outperforms hyper-segmented campaigns
- AI-driven targeting (Advantage+, Smart bidding) handles complexity better than humans
- Consolidate audiences to give algorithms more data to optimize against

### Campaign Architecture
- Fewer, better-funded campaigns outperform many underfunded ones
- Each campaign should have clear, distinct purpose (do not overlap)
- Consolidation reduces CPA by giving algorithms faster learning

### Testing Discipline
- Test one variable at a time — multiple variables create uninterpretable results
- Limit test variations to 3-5 per sprint — more becomes noise
- Declare winners and iterate rather than running open-ended tests

## Key Takeaways
- Simplicity converts — in ad design, targeting, and offer structure
- One ad, one message, one CTA, one audience objective
- Consolidate campaigns for algorithm efficiency and management clarity
- Curate choices for the user rather than overwhelming them
- The paradox of choice reminds us that less is often more in advertising
