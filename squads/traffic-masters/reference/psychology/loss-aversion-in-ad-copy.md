# Loss Aversion in Ad Copy
> **Category**: Psychology Applied to Advertising
> **Last Updated**: 2026-03

## Overview
Loss aversion is the finding that losses are psychologically about twice as powerful as equivalent gains. People will work harder to avoid losing R$100 than to gain R$100.

## Application to Ad Copy

### Gain Frame vs Loss Frame
- Gain: "Save R$200 on your next order"
- Loss: "Don't lose R$200 — offer expires tonight"
- Loss frame typically outperforms gain frame by 10-30% in CTR

### Status Quo Challenge
- "Every day without [product], you're losing [specific amount/outcome]"
- "Your competitors are already using this — are you falling behind?"
- Frames inaction as the risky choice, not the safe choice

### Opportunity Cost
- "While you're still doing [old way], others are getting [result]"
- "The cost of waiting: R$X per month in lost revenue"
- Makes the abstract cost of inaction concrete and specific

### FOMO (Fear of Missing Out)
- "2,347 people already enrolled — don't be left out"
- "Last time we opened this, it sold out in 3 hours"
- Combines loss aversion with social proof

## Copy Formulas Using Loss Aversion
1. "Stop losing [X] by [doing/not doing Y]"
2. "Don't let [problem] cost you another [specific amount]"
3. "[Number] people already [got result] — are you missing out?"
4. "Warning: [inaction] is costing you [specific consequence]"
5. "What's the real cost of not [taking action]?"

## When to Use Loss Framing
- Bottom of funnel: push hesitant buyers to decide
- Retargeting: remind abandoners what they are giving up
- Deadline-based offers: amplify urgency with loss language
- Competitive markets: position inaction as falling behind

## When NOT to Use Loss Framing
- TOFU/awareness: can feel aggressive to cold audiences
- Sensitive topics: health, financial hardship — empathy first
- Brand-building: positive framing builds better long-term association

## Key Takeaways
- Loss framing outperforms gain framing for direct response in most tests
- Make the loss specific and quantifiable for maximum impact
- Balance loss framing with positive outcomes — pure negativity exhausts
- Test loss vs gain framing for your specific audience and offer
- Use loss framing strategically, not in every ad — variety prevents fatigue
