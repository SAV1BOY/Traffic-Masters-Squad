# Thinking, Fast and Slow
> **Author**: Daniel Kahneman
> **Year**: 2011
> **Category**: Behavioral Psychology & Decision Making

## Key Concepts
- Two cognitive systems: System 1 (fast, intuitive, automatic) and System 2 (slow, deliberate, analytical)
- Most decisions, including purchase decisions, are made by System 1
- Cognitive biases are systematic errors in thinking that affect decisions
- Anchoring, loss aversion, and framing effects are exploitable in ethical marketing
- Attention is a limited resource — ads must work within its constraints

## Core Frameworks
1. **System 1 vs System 2**: Intuitive vs analytical processing
2. **Anchoring Effect**: First number seen influences all subsequent judgments
3. **Loss Aversion**: Losses feel 2x more painful than equivalent gains feel good
4. **Framing Effect**: How information is presented changes how it is processed
5. **Availability Heuristic**: What comes to mind easily is perceived as more common/important

## How It Applies to Traffic Squad
- System 1 processing explains why hooks and visuals matter more than logic in ads
- Anchoring informs pricing display strategy on landing pages
- Loss aversion drives urgency and risk-reversal messaging
- Framing effects determine whether ad copy emphasizes gain or pain
- Availability heuristic explains why brand awareness campaigns improve conversion rates

## Key Takeaways for the Squad
1. Most ad decisions happen in System 1 — design for emotion, not logic
2. Visual hooks and pattern interrupts engage System 1 instantly
3. Anchor high, then show the deal — pricing perception is relative
4. Frame offers in terms of what the user loses by not acting
5. Keep ad messaging simple — complex messages trigger System 2 (slower, more skeptical)
6. Repeated exposure (frequency) makes brands feel more familiar and trustworthy
