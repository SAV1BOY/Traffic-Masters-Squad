# How Brands Grow
> **Author**: Byron Sharp
> **Year**: 2010
> **Category**: Brand Growth & Marketing Science

## Key Concepts
- Brands grow primarily by increasing mental availability and physical availability
- Mental availability: how easily a brand comes to mind in buying situations
- Physical availability: how easy it is to find and buy the brand
- Double jeopardy law: smaller brands have fewer buyers who buy less frequently
- Reach matters more than frequency for growth; target the entire category, not segments

## Core Frameworks
1. **Mental Availability**: Build distinctive brand assets (colors, sounds, shapes)
2. **Physical Availability**: Be present everywhere buyers shop
3. **Double Jeopardy**: Market share drives both penetration and frequency
4. **Broad Reach**: Target all category buyers, not narrow segments
5. **Distinctive Assets**: Consistent visual and verbal brand elements across all ads

## How It Applies to Traffic Squad
- Challenges the hyper-targeting orthodoxy — broad audiences often win
- Supports awareness/reach campaigns that many performance marketers dismiss
- Explains why CBO with broad targeting outperforms narrow interest targeting
- Brand consistency in ads builds mental availability over time

## Key Takeaways for the Squad
1. Reach more people rather than reaching the same people more often
2. Broad targeting can outperform narrow targeting at scale
3. Brand awareness campaigns have measurable downstream effects on CPA
4. Distinctive brand assets should be consistent across all ad creatives
5. Growth comes from acquiring new buyers, not just retaining existing ones
6. Do not over-segment — most category buyers look more alike than different
