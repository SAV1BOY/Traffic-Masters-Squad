# Made to Stick
> **Author**: Chip Heath, Dan Heath
> **Year**: 2007
> **Category**: Communication & Messaging

## Key Concepts
- Why some ideas stick and others do not — the SUCCESs framework
- Sticky ideas are simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional, and story-driven
- The Curse of Knowledge: experts forget what it is like to not know
- Sticky messaging is essential for ads that are remembered and acted upon

## Core Frameworks (SUCCESs)
1. **Simple**: Find the core message — strip away everything unnecessary
2. **Unexpected**: Break patterns to grab attention; create curiosity gaps
3. **Concrete**: Use sensory, specific language instead of abstract concepts
4. **Credible**: Use proof elements (statistics, testimonials, demonstrations)
5. **Emotional**: Make people feel something — appeal to identity and values
6. **Stories**: Narratives are more memorable and persuasive than facts

## How It Applies to Traffic Squad
- SUCCESs framework is a creative brief checklist for ad production
- "Unexpected" directly maps to hook strategy in video and static ads
- "Concrete" improves ad copy by replacing vague claims with specific results
- "Stories" supports narrative-driven ad formats (UGC, testimonials, case studies)

## Key Takeaways for the Squad
1. The best hooks are unexpected — they break the pattern the viewer expects
2. Concrete numbers outperform vague claims ("Lost 12kg in 8 weeks" beats "Lose weight fast")
3. Simplicity is not dumbing down — it is finding the one core message
4. Credibility comes from specificity and proof, not from claims alone
5. Emotional ads outperform rational ads for most consumer products
6. Story-format ads have higher completion rates and recall
