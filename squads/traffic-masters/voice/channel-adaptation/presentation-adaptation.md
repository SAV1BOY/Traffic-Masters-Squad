# Voice Adaptation: Presentations

## Purpose
Guidelines for adapting the Traffic Masters voice for live and recorded presentations (QBRs, strategy reviews, campaign kickoffs, internal share-outs). Optimizes for engagement, memorability, and clear decision-making.

---

## Core Principles for Presentations

1. **One idea per slide.** If you need a second sentence to explain the slide title, split it.
2. **Show, do not tell.** Use charts, visuals, and examples instead of paragraphs.
3. **Tell a story.** Every presentation should follow a narrative arc: situation, challenge, action, result.
4. **Design for the back row.** Font sizes, chart labels, and key numbers should be visible from a distance.
5. **End with a clear ask.** Every presentation should close with a decision, approval, or next step.

---

## Presentation Voice Characteristics

| Aspect | Guideline |
|---|---|
| **Tone** | Confident, conversational, enthusiastic but grounded |
| **Person** | First person plural ("We launched...", "Our recommendation is...") |
| **Tense** | Past tense for results, present for status, future for proposals |
| **Pacing** | One slide per 1-2 minutes; 15-minute presentation = 10-15 slides max |
| **Jargon** | Minimal for client presentations; acceptable for internal |
| **Energy** | Higher than written communication; use active verbs and decisive language |

## Slide-by-Slide Voice Guide

### Title Slide
- **Voice:** Confident, clear
- **Content:** Presentation title, date, audience, presenter name
- **Title format:** Action-oriented or outcome-oriented
- **Good:** "Q1 Performance: 42% CPA Reduction and Path to Scale"
- **Avoid:** "Q1 Paid Media Update"

### Agenda Slide
- **Voice:** Direct, organized
- **Content:** 3-5 agenda items maximum
- **Pattern:** Frame agenda items as questions the presentation will answer
- **Example:** "1. How did we perform against targets? 2. What drove the results? 3. Where do we go from here?"

### Executive Summary Slide
- **Voice:** Decisive, big-picture
- **Content:** 3-5 bullet points that capture the entire story
- **Pattern:** Result + driver + recommendation
- **Example:** "Exceeded Q1 revenue target by 18%, driven by Google Shopping expansion. Recommend increasing Shopping budget 25% in Q2 to capitalize on momentum."

### KPI Dashboard Slide
- **Voice:** Visual, data-forward
- **Content:** 4-6 key metrics with trend indicators
- **Formatting:**
  - Large numbers with directional arrows or color coding
  - Green for on/above target, red for below target, yellow for within 10% of target
  - Include the target value next to each metric
- **Speaker notes:** Prepare one sentence per metric explaining the "so what"

### Performance Deep-Dive Slides
- **Voice:** Analytical but accessible
- **Content:** One chart or table per slide with a clear takeaway
- **Pattern:** Slide title IS the insight, not the topic
- **Good title:** "Meta CPA Dropped 22% After UGC Creative Launch"
- **Bad title:** "Meta Campaign Performance"
- **Speaker notes:** Include the evidence, interpretation, and implication

### Channel / Campaign Comparison Slides
- **Voice:** Comparative, evaluative
- **Content:** Side-by-side or stacked bar charts
- **Pattern:** Highlight the winner and the gap
- **Example title:** "Google Shopping Delivers 2.3x Higher ROAS Than Meta Prospecting"
- **Call out:** Use annotations or callout boxes to draw attention to key data points

### Creative Performance Slides
- **Voice:** Visual, specific
- **Content:** Creative thumbnails alongside performance data
- **Pattern:** Show the creative, state the result, explain why it worked
- **Example:** "This UGC testimonial delivered $34 CPA -- 32% below average. The authentic tone and specific result claim drove a 2.1x higher CTR than polished alternatives."

### Insights and Learnings Slides
- **Voice:** Reflective, forward-looking
- **Content:** 3-5 key learnings with implications
- **Pattern:** What we learned + what it means + what we will do differently
- **Example:** "Learning: Broad audiences outperform detailed targeting on Meta after iOS changes. Implication: Shift budget from interest-based to broad with creative-led targeting."

### Recommendations Slide
- **Voice:** Assertive, action-oriented
- **Content:** 3-5 prioritized recommendations
- **Pattern:** Recommendation + expected impact + investment required
- **Formatting:** Number recommendations by priority; include estimated impact
- **Example:** "1. Scale Google Shopping +25% ($12K/mo increase). Expected impact: +80 conversions/month at current $28 CPA."

### Next Steps Slide
- **Voice:** Clear, specific, accountable
- **Content:** Action items with owners and deadlines
- **Pattern:** What + who + when
- **Formatting:** Table or checklist format
- **Example:**

| Action | Owner | Deadline |
|---|---|---|
| Launch 4 new UGC variants | Creative Team | March 15 |
| Expand Shopping to 3 new categories | Media Team | March 20 |
| Set up incrementality test | Analytics | March 25 |

### Appendix Slides
- **Voice:** Reference, detailed
- **Content:** Supporting data, methodology notes, detailed tables
- **Note:** Do not present appendix slides unless asked; they exist for follow-up questions

## Presentation Types and Adaptations

### Quarterly Business Review (QBR)
- **Audience:** Client stakeholders, leadership
- **Length:** 30-45 minutes (20-30 slides)
- **Arc:** Results summary, what worked, what did not, strategic recommendations for next quarter
- **Tone:** Professional, strategic, partnership-oriented
- **Key phrase patterns:**
  - "This quarter, we focused on [OBJECTIVE] and achieved [RESULT]."
  - "The biggest opportunity for next quarter is [OPPORTUNITY]."
  - "We recommend [ACTION] because [EVIDENCE]."

### Campaign Kickoff
- **Audience:** Client team, internal team
- **Length:** 20-30 minutes (15-20 slides)
- **Arc:** Objectives, strategy, targeting, creative plan, measurement plan, timeline
- **Tone:** Energetic, organized, collaborative
- **Key phrase patterns:**
  - "Our primary objective is [GOAL], measured by [KPI]."
  - "The strategy centers on [APPROACH] because [RATIONALE]."
  - "We will know this is working when [SUCCESS_CRITERIA]."

### Strategy Proposal
- **Audience:** Decision-makers
- **Length:** 20-30 minutes (15-25 slides)
- **Arc:** Current state, opportunity/problem, proposed strategy, projected outcomes, investment, risks
- **Tone:** Persuasive, evidence-based, confident
- **Key phrase patterns:**
  - "The current approach is delivering [RESULT]. We see an opportunity to [IMPROVEMENT]."
  - "Based on [DATA], we propose [STRATEGY]."
  - "The projected impact is [OUTCOME] with an investment of [AMOUNT]."

### Internal Share-Out
- **Audience:** Internal team
- **Length:** 10-15 minutes (8-12 slides)
- **Arc:** What happened, what we learned, what the team should know
- **Tone:** Casual, educational, direct
- **Key phrase patterns:**
  - "Here is what happened and why it matters."
  - "The key takeaway is [LEARNING]."
  - "If you are working on something similar, consider [ADVICE]."

### Performance Alert / Escalation
- **Audience:** Leadership, client
- **Length:** 5-10 minutes (5-8 slides)
- **Arc:** What happened, impact, root cause, actions taken, resolution timeline
- **Tone:** Calm, factual, solution-focused
- **Key phrase patterns:**
  - "We identified [ISSUE] on [DATE]. Here is the current status."
  - "The root cause is [CAUSE]. We have taken [ACTIONS]."
  - "We expect resolution by [DATE]."

## Data Visualization Guidelines

### Chart Selection
| Data Type | Recommended Chart | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Trend over time | Line chart | Pie chart |
| Comparison across categories | Horizontal bar chart | 3D charts |
| Part of whole | Stacked bar or donut chart | Multiple pie charts |
| Correlation | Scatter plot | Tables with many rows |
| Single KPI | Large number with trend arrow | Embedding in a paragraph |

### Chart Formatting
- **Title:** State the insight, not the data description
- **Colors:** Use brand colors; limit to 4-5 colors per chart
- **Labels:** Include data labels on key values; remove gridlines where possible
- **Annotations:** Add callout boxes for key inflection points or events
- **Simplify:** Remove chart junk -- unnecessary borders, backgrounds, legends when only 1-2 series

### Numbers on Slides
- Round aggressively: $1.2M not $1,234,567
- Use the largest readable unit: 125K not 125,000
- Percentages: whole numbers unless the decimal matters (12% not 12.3%)
- Always include the comparison: "$42 CPA (target: $50)" not just "$42 CPA"

## Speaker Notes Guidelines

### Structure
- **Opening line:** What to say when this slide appears
- **Key points:** 2-3 talking points in bullet form
- **Transition:** How to bridge to the next slide
- **Backup data:** Numbers or details to reference if asked

### Language in Speaker Notes
- Write in natural spoken language, not formal prose
- Use contractions ("we've seen" not "we have observed")
- Include pause points: "[PAUSE]" before revealing a key number
- Include interaction prompts: "[ASK] Any questions before we move to recommendations?"

### Example Speaker Notes
```
[SLIDE: Meta CPA Dropped 22% After UGC Creative Launch]

OPENING: "This is the biggest win of the quarter."

KEY POINTS:
- UGC creative launched on Feb 12. Within 5 days, CPA dropped from $58 to $45.
- The winning variant was the customer testimonial filmed on iPhone -- authentic feel, specific results mentioned.
- This reinforces what we've been seeing across accounts: authenticity beats production value for cold audiences.

TRANSITION: "This learning directly informs our creative strategy for next quarter, which I'll cover next."

BACKUP: Full creative test data is in appendix slide 4.
```

## Presentation Delivery Tips

### Pacing
- Spend more time on insight slides than data slides
- Move quickly through context-setting; slow down for recommendations
- Budget 5 minutes for Q&A per 15 minutes of content

### Audience Engagement
- Ask a question in the first 2 minutes to establish dialogue
- Pause after sharing a key metric -- let it land
- Reference something the audience said or shared previously to show you are listening
- Use "you" language for clients: "your customers," "your revenue," "your growth"

### Handling Questions
- Repeat the question before answering
- If you do not know: "Great question. Let me confirm the exact number and follow up by end of day."
- If the question is off-topic: "That is an important point. Can we capture it and circle back after we finish the main presentation?"
- If challenged on data: "Let me walk through the methodology" (reference appendix)

## Presentation Quality Checklist

- [ ] Title slide includes date, audience, and presenter
- [ ] Slide titles state insights, not topics
- [ ] No slide has more than 6 bullet points or 30 words of body text
- [ ] Every chart has a clear takeaway visible without explanation
- [ ] Numbers are rounded and include comparison points
- [ ] Recommendations are specific with expected impact
- [ ] Next steps have owners and deadlines
- [ ] Speaker notes are complete for every content slide
- [ ] Appendix includes supporting detail for anticipated questions
- [ ] Presentation has been rehearsed at least once
- [ ] Total slide count matches the allocated time (1-2 minutes per slide)
