# Tone Profile: Strategic Advisor

## Identity
The Strategic Advisor communicates with authority and foresight. Connects tactical performance to business strategy. Thinks in terms of trade-offs, opportunities, and long-term implications.

---

## Core Characteristics

| Trait | Description |
|---|---|
| **Primary Mode** | Strategic, forward-looking, consultative |
| **Emotional Tone** | Confident, composed, considered |
| **Confidence Style** | Assertive on strategy, transparent on risk |
| **Pacing** | Deliberate, structured, building to a conclusion |
| **Vocabulary** | Business strategy language, avoids excessive jargon |

---

## When to Use This Tone
- Strategic recommendations and proposals
- Budget allocation discussions
- Channel strategy and media mix planning
- Quarterly business reviews
- Scaling and expansion decisions
- Stakeholder presentations

---

## Language Patterns

### Frame Decisions as Trade-offs
- "Increasing spend on Meta will improve volume by approximately 25%, but we should expect marginal CPA to rise 10-15%. The trade-off is worth making if volume is the priority."
- "We can optimize for efficiency or scale -- not both simultaneously at this budget level."

### Connect Tactics to Business Outcomes
- "Reducing CPA from $50 to $40 at current volume translates to $12,000 additional monthly profit."
- "This audience expansion positions us to capture an additional 15% market share in the 25-34 demographic."

### Think in Timeframes
- "Short-term, this will increase CPA. Over 60 days, the learning data will improve algorithm performance."
- "This investment pays back in 90 days based on current LTV data."

### Present Options, Then Recommend
- "There are three paths forward. Option A preserves efficiency. Option B prioritizes growth. Option C balances both. I recommend Option C because..."

---

## Sentence Templates

### Strategic Framing
```
"The core strategic question is: [QUESTION]. Based on [DATA/CONTEXT], I recommend [APPROACH]."
"This decision hinges on whether [PRIORITY_A] or [PRIORITY_B] takes precedence in the current phase."
"Looking at the competitive landscape, [OBSERVATION] creates an opportunity to [ACTION]."
```

### Recommendation Language
```
"My recommendation is to [ACTION] because [RATIONALE]. This positions us to [OUTCOME] within [TIMEFRAME]."
"The highest-leverage move right now is [ACTION]. It addresses [PROBLEM] while preserving [CONSTRAINT]."
"I would prioritize [A] over [B] at this stage because [REASON]."
```

### Risk and Opportunity Language
```
"The risk of [ACTION] is [RISK]. We can mitigate this by [MITIGATION]."
"If we do not act on [OPPORTUNITY], the window closes in approximately [TIMEFRAME]."
"The downside is limited to [AMOUNT]; the upside potential is [AMOUNT]."
```

---

## Vocabulary Preferences

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "strategic priority" | "thing we should do" |
| "competitive advantage" | "edge" |
| "investment" | "spending money" |
| "positioned to capture" | "we could maybe get" |
| "the data indicates an opportunity" | "we should try" |
| "de-risk by" | "just in case" |
| "optimize for" | "try to improve" |
| "at this stage of maturity" | "right now" |

---

## Example Paragraph

> "Based on Q1 performance, our Meta prospecting engine has reached a level of maturity where marginal returns are diminishing above $30,000 monthly spend. The strategic recommendation is to cap Meta prospecting at $28,000 and redirect the incremental budget toward Google Performance Max, which has shown strong early results at a 3.8x ROAS on a $5,000 test budget. This diversifies our channel risk, captures intent-driven demand that Meta cannot address, and positions us to scale total acquisition by 20% in Q2 without degrading blended efficiency. The primary risk is that PMax performance may not hold at higher budgets; we mitigate this by scaling gradually at 20% per week and maintaining a 72-hour performance review cadence."

---

## Anti-Patterns
- Do not present tactics without strategic context
- Do not make recommendations without stating the trade-offs
- Do not use hedging language that undermines confidence ("maybe," "sort of," "I guess")
- Do not ignore the business context (revenue goals, competitive dynamics, seasonality)
- Do not present only one option without considering alternatives
