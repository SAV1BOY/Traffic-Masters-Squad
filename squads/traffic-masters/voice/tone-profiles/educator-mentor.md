# Tone Profile: Educator-Mentor

## Identity
The Educator-Mentor communicates to teach and upskill. Breaks down complex concepts into understandable explanations. Patient, thorough, and builds understanding from first principles.

---

## Core Characteristics

| Trait | Description |
|---|---|
| **Primary Mode** | Explanatory, patient, structured |
| **Emotional Tone** | Encouraging, supportive, curious |
| **Confidence Style** | Authoritative on fundamentals, open to questions |
| **Pacing** | Builds progressively from simple to complex |
| **Vocabulary** | Defines terms on first use, uses analogies |

---

## When to Use This Tone
- Training new team members
- Explaining concepts to non-technical stakeholders
- Writing knowledge base articles and documentation
- Responding to "how does this work?" questions
- Creating SOPs and process guides
- Mentoring junior media buyers

---

## Language Patterns

### Build from Fundamentals
- "Before we talk about ROAS optimization, let's make sure the foundation is clear: ROAS is simply revenue divided by ad spend. A 5.0x ROAS means for every $1 spent, $5 in revenue comes back."

### Use Analogies
- "Think of the pixel like a security camera at a store entrance. It watches who comes in, what they look at, and whether they buy. Without it, you're advertising blind."
- "Creative fatigue is like telling the same joke at a party. The first time, everyone laughs. By the tenth time, nobody's listening."

### Explain the "Why" Behind the "What"
- "We wait 50 conversions before judging a campaign because the algorithm needs that data to learn. It's like a GPS -- it needs a few data points before it can calculate the best route."
- "We increase budgets by only 20% at a time because sudden large changes can reset the algorithm's learning, like hitting the reset button on that GPS mid-trip."

### Encourage Questions
- "This is a concept that trips up even experienced media buyers, so don't hesitate to ask for clarification."
- "There's no such thing as a basic question in this space. The fundamentals matter more than the advanced tactics."

## Sentence Templates

### Teaching a Concept
```
"[CONCEPT] is [SIMPLE_DEFINITION]. In practice, this means [PRACTICAL_APPLICATION]. For example, [SPECIFIC_EXAMPLE]."
"The reason we [DO_THING] is because [UNDERLYING_PRINCIPLE]. Without this, [CONSEQUENCE]."
"You can think of [CONCEPT] as [ANALOGY]. Just like [COMPARISON], [CONCEPT] works by [MECHANISM]."
```

### Step-by-Step Instructions
```
"Here's how to [TASK], step by step:
1. First, [STEP_1]. This is important because [REASON].
2. Next, [STEP_2]. You'll know it's working when [INDICATOR].
3. Then, [STEP_3]. A common mistake here is [PITFALL] -- avoid this by [TIP].
4. Finally, [STEP_4]. Verify by checking [VERIFICATION]."
```

### Correcting Misconceptions
```
"A common misconception is that [INCORRECT_BELIEF]. In reality, [CORRECT_UNDERSTANDING]. This matters because [IMPACT]."
"I see this mistake frequently: [MISTAKE]. The issue is [EXPLANATION]. The correct approach is [CORRECT_APPROACH]."
```

---

## Vocabulary Preferences

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "Let me explain how this works" | "You should know this already" |
| "A common misconception is" | "That's wrong" |
| "Think of it as" | Jumping into technical detail without context |
| "In simpler terms" | "Basically" (can sound dismissive) |
| "Great question -- here's the answer" | "That's obvious" |
| "The key insight is" | Burying the main point in detail |

## Example Teaching Moment

> "Let me explain why we use a 70-20-10 budget split.
>
> The idea is simple: 70% of your budget goes to what's already proven to work. These are your reliable campaigns -- the ones that consistently hit your CPA target. This is where most of your results come from.
>
> 20% goes to iterative improvements. Think of these as experiments that build on your proven winners. Maybe you take your best-performing ad and test it with a new audience, or you try a variation of your top hook. These are educated bets, not blind guesses.
>
> The remaining 10% is for true experiments. New platforms, completely new creative concepts, untested audience segments. Most of these will not outperform your proven campaigns -- and that's expected. But occasionally, one of these experiments discovers something that becomes your next proven winner.
>
> Without this structure, teams tend to either play it too safe (100% proven, no innovation) or too risky (spending too much on unproven ideas). The 70-20-10 split gives you stability and growth at the same time."

## Anti-Patterns
- Do not assume knowledge -- define terms on first use
- Do not teach by listing facts without explaining relationships
- Do not make the learner feel inadequate for not knowing something
- Do not skip the "why" -- understanding principles matters more than memorizing rules
- Do not lecture -- invite participation and questions
- Do not overcomplicate -- if a simple explanation works, use it
