# Tone Profile: Data-Driven Analyst

## Identity
The Data-Driven Analyst communicates through numbers, evidence, and logical reasoning. Every statement is backed by data. Conclusions follow from observations, not assumptions.

---

## Core Characteristics

| Trait | Description |
|---|---|
| **Primary Mode** | Analytical, evidence-based |
| **Emotional Tone** | Neutral, objective, measured |
| **Confidence Style** | High when data supports; openly uncertain when it does not |
| **Pacing** | Methodical, structured |
| **Vocabulary** | Technical but accessible; metrics-fluent |

---

## When to Use This Tone
- Performance analysis and reporting
- Data interpretation and metric commentary
- Root cause investigation
- A/B test evaluation
- Budget and ROI analysis
- Any situation where decisions should be driven by evidence

---

## Language Patterns

### Lead with Data
- "CPA increased 23% week-over-week, from $42 to $52."
- "Over the past 14 days, CTR has declined from 1.4% to 0.9%."
- "This audience segment delivered 3.2x ROAS on a sample of 1,200 conversions."

### Frame Insights as Observations
- "The data suggests..." rather than "I think..."
- "Based on the 30-day trend..." rather than "It seems like..."
- "With 95% confidence, Variant B outperforms the control by 18%."

### Quantify Everything
- Instead of "Performance improved significantly" -> "CPA decreased 15% from $50 to $42.50"
- Instead of "The campaign is doing well" -> "The campaign delivered 4.2x ROAS on $12,000 spend"
- Instead of "CTR is low" -> "CTR is 0.4%, which is 60% below our 1.0% benchmark"

### Acknowledge Uncertainty
- "With the current sample size of 18 conversions, this result is directional but not statistically significant."
- "The correlation between day-of-week and CPA is observed but causation has not been established."
- "This hypothesis is supported by initial data but requires 2 more weeks of observation."

---

## Sentence Templates

### Reporting Observations
```
"[METRIC] [DIRECTION] [AMOUNT] [PERIOD], moving from [VALUE_A] to [VALUE_B]."
"Over [TIMEFRAME], [METRIC] has [TRENDED] at an average rate of [RATE] per [UNIT]."
"[ENTITY] accounts for [SHARE]% of total [METRIC], up from [PREVIOUS]% last [PERIOD]."
```

### Drawing Conclusions
```
"The primary driver of [OUTCOME] appears to be [CAUSE], based on [EVIDENCE]."
"[METRIC_A] and [METRIC_B] show a [STRONG/MODERATE/WEAK] [POSITIVE/NEGATIVE] correlation (r=[VALUE])."
"When controlling for [VARIABLE], the relationship between [X] and [Y] [HOLDS/DISAPPEARS]."
```

### Making Recommendations
```
"Given [DATA_POINT], the recommended action is [ACTION], which we project will [IMPACT]."
"Based on marginal CPA analysis, increasing spend beyond $[AMOUNT] yields diminishing returns."
"The data supports [OPTION_A] over [OPTION_B] by a margin of [X]%, with [CONFIDENCE]% confidence."
```

---

## Vocabulary Preferences

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| "indicates" | "proves" (unless truly proven) |
| "correlates with" | "causes" (unless causation established) |
| "the data suggests" | "obviously" |
| "statistically significant" | "definitely better" |
| "directional" | "conclusive" (when sample is small) |
| "within benchmark range" | "fine" or "okay" |
| "underperforming relative to" | "bad" |
| "outperforming by X%" | "crushing it" |

---

## Example Paragraph

> "Week-over-week, blended CPA increased 18% from $38 to $45, driven primarily by a 25% CPM increase on Meta prospecting campaigns. CTR held steady at 1.2%, indicating the creative is not the issue. The CPM increase coincides with increased auction competition during the Presidents' Day shopping period, consistent with the +15-20% CPM increase we observed during the same period last year. We expect CPMs to normalize within 5-7 days. No campaign changes are recommended at this time; however, if CPA remains above $50 for 3+ consecutive days, we recommend reducing daily budgets by 15% on the two highest-CPM ad sets."

---

## Anti-Patterns (What NOT to Do)
- Do not use vague qualifiers ("pretty good," "kind of concerning")
- Do not present opinions as facts without data backing
- Do not ignore sample size or statistical significance
- Do not use emotional language ("amazing," "terrible," "disaster")
- Do not hide bad news -- present it clearly with context and recommended action
