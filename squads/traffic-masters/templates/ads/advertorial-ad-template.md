# Advertorial Ad Template

> **Type**: Template
> **Category**: ads
> **Used by tasks**: advertorial-creation, native-ad-campaigns, content-marketing
> **Filled by agents**: copywriter-agent, creative-strategist-agent

## Purpose
Structures advertorial-style ads and landing pages that blend editorial content with promotional messaging, creating a native reading experience that builds trust before presenting the offer.

## Template

### Advertorial Metadata
**Platform**: [Meta / Google Display / Taboola / Outbrain / native placement]
**Format**: [article page / in-feed ad / sponsored content]
**Target audience awareness level**: [problem-aware / solution-aware]
**Word count target**: [800-1500 words for full advertorial page]
**Reading time**: [3-6 minutes]

### Editorial Headline
**Headline A (Curiosity)**: "[Newsworthy angle that reads like a real article]"
**Headline B (Discovery)**: "[Personal story format: 'How I discovered...']"
**Headline C (Data-driven)**: "[Research or study angle: 'New study reveals...']"
**Rules**:
- Must not look or feel like an ad headline
- Avoid superlatives, ALL CAPS, or excessive punctuation
- Use title case, not sentence case
- Match the editorial style of the publication or platform

### Subheadline
**Subheadline**: "[Expands on the headline with a specific detail or benefit]"

### Story / Problem Section
**Opening paragraph**: "[Relatable scenario or personal anecdote that draws the reader in. Establish the character and their struggle.]"
**Problem development**: "[2-3 paragraphs deepening the problem. Include statistics, research, or expert quotes that validate the issue.]"
**Emotional hook**: "[The moment of frustration, realization, or desperation that makes the reader nod in agreement.]"
**Word count**: [300-500 words]
**Tone**: Journalistic, empathetic, informative. Not salesy.

### Bridge / Discovery
**Transition paragraph**: "[Natural shift from problem to solution. 'That's when I/they discovered...' or 'Researchers found that...']"
**Notes**: This is the critical pivot. If it feels like an ad, the reader leaves.

### Solution Section
**Solution introduction**: "[Present the product/service as part of the story, not as a pitch. Describe what it does and how it works.]"
**Key differentiators**:
1. [What makes it unique - presented as editorial observation]
2. [How it addresses the specific problem discussed above]
3. [Why it works when other approaches fail]
**Expert or founder quote**: "[Include a quote that adds credibility]"
**Word count**: [200-400 words]

### Proof Stack
**Proof element 1**: "[Customer story or testimonial woven into the narrative]"
**Proof element 2**: "[Data point, study result, or clinical evidence]"
**Proof element 3**: "[Before/after or case study summary]"
**Proof element 4**: "[Media mention, award, or expert endorsement]"
**Presentation**: Integrate proof naturally into the article flow. Avoid bulleted testimonial blocks that break the editorial feel.

### Soft CTA
**In-content CTA**: "[Subtle mention with link. 'You can learn more at [site]' or 'They're currently offering [offer] for new customers.']"
**End-of-article CTA**: "[Slightly more direct. 'If you're dealing with [problem], [product] might be worth a look. Right now they have [offer].']"
**CTA button (optional)**: [text and placement]
**Rules**:
- Maximum 2 CTAs in the full article
- First CTA appears no earlier than 60% through the content
- CTA should feel like a helpful recommendation, not a sales push

### Disclosure
**Disclosure text**: "This article contains sponsored content" or "Paid partnership with [brand]"
**Placement**: [top of page / bottom of page / both]
**Legal requirements**: [FTC guidelines, platform-specific rules]
**Notes**: Disclosure is legally required. Place it where it satisfies regulations without dominating the reading experience.

### Design Guidelines
**Layout**: Clean, editorial. Single-column text, generous white space.
**Typography**: Serif or clean sans-serif, 16-18px body text.
**Images**: Editorial photography style, not product catalog shots.
**Pull quotes**: Use 1-2 to break up text and highlight key insights.
**Navigation**: Minimal. No sidebar ads or distracting elements.
**Mobile**: Must be fully responsive; most traffic will be mobile.

### Ad Creative (for driving traffic to the advertorial)
**Ad headline**: [Match editorial headline style]
**Ad image**: [Curiosity-driven, editorial photo, not product shot]
**Ad text**: [Brief teaser that makes the reader want the full story]
**CTA on ad**: [Read More / Learn More / See the Story]

## Usage Notes
- Advertorials work best for problem-aware to solution-aware audiences.
- A/B test headlines aggressively; they determine 80% of click-through rate.
- Monitor time-on-page and scroll depth as engagement metrics.
- Ensure the landing page loads fast; editorial readers abandon slow pages.

## Example
A supplement brand creates an article titled "The Overlooked Mineral Deficiency Behind Chronic Fatigue" that educates on magnesium, cites research, includes a doctor's quote, and naturally introduces their product as one solution among others.
